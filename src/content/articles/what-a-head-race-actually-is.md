Your kid's first fall as a novice rower, and someone says "head race" like you already know what that means. You picture spring sprint racing: six boats in lanes, a start gun, everyone flying for about six minutes.

That is not what fall looks like.

**A head race is a time trial on a long course.** High school and club head races commonly run 4,000 to 5,000 meters, about two to three times the spring championship distance of 2,000 meters. Boats leave the start one after another, often ten to twenty seconds apart, and race the clock instead of a neighboring lane.

The result sheet is ordered by elapsed time, not by who crossed a shared finish line first in a pack.

USRowing's youth calendar treats fall and spring as different seasons with different shapes. Fall builds aerobic base through those long pieces. Spring is the championship season: sprint regattas, regionals, and for the boats that qualify, Youth Nationals.

**What you see from the bank feels wrong if you came from track or swimming finals.** There is no clean six-lane blast off a shared gun. One eight goes by, then a gap, then another boat with a coxswain calling something you cannot quite hear.

You will not know if your kid's boat is "winning" until the times post. Bring coffee and patience.

**Head of the Charles is the famous one, not the typical one.** It lands late October in Boston and draws invited boats from around the country. Most high school programs race smaller regional head races first.

Your novice boat may never see the Charles. That is normal. The format still matters because September practice is already shaping pieces around that longer distance.

**Why coaches start novices here.** A kid who picked up an oar in August is not ready for a full spring sprint season's worth of boat-moving precision. Fall head-race training gives them long, steady kilometers to learn the catch, the finish, and sitting the boat without the lane-race pressure of a 2,000-meter final.

The racing still counts. The result still goes on a sheet. The point of the season is base, not a state title.

If you want the year-round map, [HS crew spring racing with fall head races](/season-calendar/hs-crew-spring-racing/) lays out the months. For the early-morning part of this sport that nobody warns you about, read [crew at 5 a.m.](/team-parent/crew-5am-different-clock-family/).

Next Saturday, when the first boat pushes off alone and the next one waits on the stake boats, you will know you are watching a head race. Not a sprint with a confusing start. A different event on purpose.
