Your kid has played travel ball since nine. The schedule got harder every year. School got harder.

They want to step away from travel. You agree. The hard part is doing it cleanly.

## Finish the season, then say it plain

Finish the season if health and safety allow it. The coach planned the roster around them.

If the season is unbearable, talk early: they are overcommitted, you will manage attendance down, and you still intend to finish. Many coaches will work with a clear plan.

If the conflict is a full mid-season exit, use the [mid-season quit call](/drive-home/the-13-to-14-quit-mid-season-call/) rather than disappearing.

After the final game, schedule a short call or in-person chat.

Thank the coach for the season. Say you are not returning next year because the schedule got too big with school.

Keep it short and polite. Do not ghost re-registration and hope the coach figures it out.

## Keep the exit clean

Leave parking-lot politics out of the goodbye. The coach you did not love and the booster fight belong in a later note, if at all.

Other travel parents will ask why you are leaving. Use the same line every time: the schedule got too big, and you are prioritizing other things.

A thank-you to the coach preserves options for school ball, guest play, and future recommendations. Youth sports worlds are small.

Let your kid choose the social exit. Some write a thank-you. Some text teammates.

Some want a quiet fade. All three can be reasonable.

## Name the next season first

Have a plan before the exit goes public: school ball, rec, pickup, or another sport. The plan makes the exit feel intentional.

If you are still deciding whether travel was the wrong fit, read [when you say no to travel](/drive-home/when-you-say-no-to-travel/) and [walking away from travel at 16](/drive-home/walking-away-from-travel-ball-at-16/).

Sometimes the kid wants to continue and the family cannot sustain it. Be honest: we cannot continue this travel commitment. Own the family call.

They can leave travel and keep the sport. If they are done with both, treat that as its own conversation.

Keep the exit short, then walk away with grace.
