Practice opens August 4 under WIAA rules this year. The dead period, no contact between coaches and players, ends July 25. Ten days later the whistle blows, and a kid without the right forms on file does not step on the field, no matter how fast he ran his 40 this summer.

Four documents have to be signed before that whistle. Miss one and the kid sits out day one while a parent scrambles to fix it from the parking lot.

## The physical exam

WIAA requires a physical exam, cleared by a medical authority, before an athlete's first practice. It runs 24 months, not one year, which catches first-time football parents who are used to signing off on an annual physical for other sports.

The timing matters. A physical dated April 1 or later covers that school year and the one after it. A physical dated before April 1 only covers the rest of that year plus the following year.

In the off year, when a new physical isn't due, most districts still want a signed alternate-year health status card back from a parent.

The WIAA physical form runs several pages, history questions, exam boxes, a doctor's signature. Districts running paperwork through FinalForms only want the medical eligibility page uploaded, not the whole packet. Check which page before you scan the wrong one in.

No physical on file, no field. Simple as that.

## The concussion and head injury form

Athlete and parent both sign this once a year, before the first practice, not after the first hit. The signature puts the return-to-play rule in writing before anyone needs it: any suspected concussion means immediate removal, and no return without written clearance from a licensed healthcare provider.

A coach can't waive it. Neither can a parent who happens to also be running the sideline.

## The sudden cardiac arrest form

Washington's Sudden Cardiac Arrest Awareness Act, RCW 28A.600.195, requires the same kind of signature every year before an athlete participates. Athlete and parent both confirm they reviewed the state's pamphlet on sudden cardiac arrest in young athletes.

Roughly one in 250 young athletes carries an undiagnosed heart condition that raises the risk. That's the number lawmakers cited in 2015 when they passed the bill, known by its short title as the sudden cardiac arrest awareness act.

## The emergency card and the code of conduct

Every district wants current emergency contact and medical authorization on file before a kid can be treated on the field if something goes wrong. Most also require a signed athletic code of conduct, the document spelling out eligibility, attendance expectations, and what gets a player suspended from the team.

Seattle, Kelso, Puyallup, Everett, and Federal Way school districts run all four documents through the same online system, FinalForms. Check your own district's athletics page instead of assuming a paper folder still handles it.

Most districts also require a 2.0 GPA and no failing grades. That one isn't signed. It shows up on the transcript instead, and it benches a kid just as fast as a missing form.

## Before August 4

Equipment issue and fitness testing can start the same day as practice, so there is no cushion built into the calendar for paperwork that's still pending.

The first scrimmage lands August 12. The first real game follows on August 20, which means a kid who loses three or four days chasing down a signature is already behind on contact reps before the pads come out for real.

Call the athletic office this week if the login hasn't happened yet. The forms take fifteen minutes. Missing them costs a kid the first day of camp, and coaching staffs remember who showed up ready.
