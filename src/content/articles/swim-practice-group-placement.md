Short course season opens in September for most club swim teams, and the group placements coaches announce that first week land harder on families than expected. Parents hear terms like developmental group or age group silver and assume it's a ranking. That assumption is wrong.

Coaches build a group around four things: recorded times, meet attendance, stroke technique, and how a kid trains, and raw speed on one good day barely factors in. A ten-year-old with three B times and sloppy turns stays in the same group as a swimmer half a second slower with a clean stroke, and the clean stroke is the better long-term bet. Ask the coach directly and most will say exactly that.

Group moves happen at season boundaries, not mid-week on a whim. USA Swimming clubs shuffle rosters at the end of each competitive season: spring in late March, summer in August, winter around New Year's. September's short course opening is one of those windows, which is why the new-group email lands the same week as the new schedule.

Coaches lean conservative on purpose. Bumping a swimmer up before they're ready costs more than it gains: a demotion the following season hits harder than staying at the same level ever would. A coach who holds your kid back a level isn't punishing them, they're protecting them from a worse outcome down the line.

The change parents actually feel is the calendar, not the label on the group. Moving from a four-day group to a six-day group adds two more trips to the pool and another twenty to thirty minutes per practice at most clubs. Before agreeing to a placement, ask for the group's real practice schedule, not the name on the group.

Two situations are worth a real question: a kid who has hit the group's posted time standard three meets running and still hasn't moved, or a kid whose attendance and attitude are clearly fine while the coach can't say why they're staying put. Vague answers are the signal to watch for. A coach with real criteria can explain a hold in one sentence, not a shrug.

Most placements are right the first time. When one isn't, skip the club board and ask the coach directly: what does the kid need to do to move up.
