The first dual on the schedule is not the first deadline that can stop your kid from wrestling.

Before anyone steps on a mat that counts, most high school associations require a certified weight-management pass: hydration test, body-composition assessment, and entry into the state's online system. Fail the paperwork window and the dual meet still happens. Your kid sits.

**What NFHS actually requires.** NFHS Wrestling Rule 1-5 tells each state high school association to run a weight-control program that involves the wrestler, parents or guardians, appropriate health-care professionals, and the coach.

The program is supposed to set a minimum certified weight class through hydration testing, body fat assessment, and a monitored descent plan. That is the national floor. Your state's handbook fills in the dates, the approved assessors, and the software.

**October is assessment season in a lot of states.** Iowa's 2026-27 window opens October 19. Georgia's initial window runs mid-October through mid-December, with an earliest competition date in early November.

Texas UIL lists October 1 as the first day for minimum weight certification and November 9 as the first day for duals and tournaments. Idaho requires hydration and weight certification before the first allowable competition date in mid-November.

Your state will not match those dates line for line. The shape matches: assessment opens weeks before the first dual, and competition eligibility waits on a completed, entered assessment.

**Ask the AD for three numbers this week.** Write down the first day assessments may be official, the last day your coach wants the roster done before the opener, and the name of the approved assessor or site the school uses.

If the coach already booked a team night, put it on the shared calendar like a game. Do not wait for the first dual packet to explain this. That packet arrives after the window has already started.

**Hydration comes first, then the scale, then body composition.** State handbooks (Iowa's summary is typical) sequence it the same way: pass hydration, weigh in for the alpha weight, then run the body-composition assessment.

Fail hydration and the other two steps stop. Iowa requires at least 24 hours before a retest. Georgia's handbook uses a urine specific gravity ceiling of 1.025, the same NFHS Rule 1-5 figure.

Dehydrating on purpose before assessment day is a common bad idea. It fails the hydration screen, burns a retest day, and can skew the composition result.

Hydrate normally in the days before. Ask the coach what the assessor wants kids to eat and drink the night before if the school sends a tip sheet.

**Alpha roster beats the dual schedule.** Many associations use TrackWrestling's Optimal Performance Calculator (OPC) or an equivalent. Until the assessor enters the results and the wrestler's name sits on the school's alpha master roster, that wrestler is not eligible to compete at any level.

Late joiners still need the assessment before their first match. Most associations allow only one official assessment unless they grant a formal written appeal after a challenge process nobody enjoys.

Show up for the booked team night. That night decides whether your kid can wrestle in week one.

**Descent plan is a calendar, not a dare.** After assessment, the system builds a descent plan that caps how fast weight may drop week to week. Iowa's 2026-27 summary uses an average of no more than 1.5 percent of body weight per week, and it names the earliest date a wrestler may weigh in for a given class.

Your state's number may match or differ. The point for parents is simpler: the plan is paperwork with dates, not a permission slip to cut hard at home.

We do not give cutting advice. If a coach or teammate talks about skipping meals or sweating off water to "make" a class, send that conversation to the coach and athletic trainer with the certified plan in hand.

**Sports physical is a different stamp.** School athletic clearance lives in the AD's medical file. Wrestling weight-management assessment lives in the online weight system and on the alpha roster printout the coach carries.

A signed physical never places a kid on the alpha roster by itself. Clear both stamps. Then call the season ready.

**Skin checks and singlet fit still matter later.** Dual-meet mornings add skin checks and the weigh-in ritual, and those mornings feel clinical in a way parents forget until they are standing in the hallway with a snug singlet in a gym bag.

Certification lives in October and early November. Miss that window and the first dual that counts happens without your kid on the card.

**Map November against the rest of the house.** Choir nights, basketball tryouts, and club travel already fight for the same weekends. Put wrestling's assessment night and first dual on the shared family calendar this week, then negotiate every conflict in daylight before anyone books a hotel.

**One parent action before the first dual.** Email or text the coach today: "What is our assessment date, who is the approved assessor, and when do you need every wrestler entered in the system?"

Save the reply. That reply is the season-open calendar.
