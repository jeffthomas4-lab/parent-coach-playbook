The belt test is on the school calendar for a Saturday in December. The form that gets your kid onto the mat is due sometime in November, and nobody texts you a reminder.

Our [martial arts season calendar](/season-calendar/year-round-martial-arts-belts/) marks early December as a testing window, before many schools close for the holidays. That timing squeezes the paperwork into the weeks around Thanksgiving.

**The test date is the last date, not the first.** Look at how real schools post it. KC Taekwondo in Parker, Colorado, lists a December 19 test and adds a $20 late fee to any application received after the Thursday before.

Mireu Martial Arts in Coralville, Iowa, tests on December 12. Kids must pass a pre-test first, and the application goes in along with a house-chores sheet and a volunteer sheet.

Edge Martial Arts in Westchester holds an optional pre-test on November 22, two weeks before its December 6 test. Formal tests there happen twice a year. Invitations go out two to three months ahead.

**An eligibility list is not a promise.** Quest Martial Arts says it directly: being on the testing board does not guarantee a student will test. Attendance counts too, often a set number of classes over a set number of months.

Miss three weeks for a fall sport or a family trip, and your kid may get moved to the next window. At a twice-a-year school, that is May. Ask now, not at the door.

**Know the fee and how it gets paid.** Jung's Tae Kwon Do in Cedar Rapids, Iowa, posts $45 for colored belts and more for black belt ranks. Quest charges its fee to the card on file the night of the test.

Neither is unusual. Both are worth knowing before the charge lands in the same month as holiday shopping, and our [belt mill piece](/drive-there/belt-mill-or-legitimate-school-the-advanced-level-test/) covers what a fair test fee should buy.

**Make one call this week.** Ask the front desk four things: is my kid on the list, is there a pre-test, when is the form due, and how is the fee charged. Write the answers on the family calendar next to the test date.

Then let the test belong to your kid. If you feel the nerves building anyway, [our belt test nerves piece](/team-parent/belt-test-nerves-are-yours-not-kids/) explains why that knot is probably yours.
