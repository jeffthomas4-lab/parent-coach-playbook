CPSC's own guidelines document counts at least 21 deaths tied to unanchored soccer goals between 1979 and 1994, most of them not during a game. The goal was standing unattended when someone climbed it or leaned on the crossbar. That's the exact moment a post-game breakdown crew walks away from every week: net still on, goal still upright, nobody watching it.

## Secure the goal before you touch anything else

Fold the goal face down and lock it to its base, or chain it to a fence post, a dugout, or the other goal. Either satisfies CPSC's own guidance. Leaving it standing and unanchored does not, no matter how tired the crew is or how dark it's getting.

Pull the net off first. CPSC lists that as its own separate step, not a footnote to anchoring, because a goal with the net still attached gives a kid something to climb.

## One bag, one name

Team equipment bag, cones, corner flags, water jug, first-aid kit. Assign one parent to each item before the season starts, not after something turns up missing in week three.

A rotating crew of "whoever's still standing" loses track of who has what by October. A named owner for each bag doesn't.

## Assign the crew before kickoff, not after the final whistle

Two or three volunteers per game, confirmed by midweek and reminded the day before, clear a field faster than a scramble of tired parents packing up mid-conversation. Put the rotation on the same sign-up sheet you already use for snacks.

## Walk it once before the last car leaves

Check for holes, a loose base, cones left near the lot, anything the next team's crew or the rec department would flag. If your league already runs a lock-up checklist, that's the moment to use it.

Whoever locks the gate signs off that the goal is down, the net is off, and the field is clear. That's the actual job. Packing the cooler fast doesn't count if the goal's still standing behind you.
