You're driving home from practice. Your kid is in the back seat, hair smashed flat on one side, picking at a hole in his sock.

He says, *Dad. I don't think I'm a good soccer player.*

Nobody hands parents a script for the back seat at 6:15pm on a Tuesday. Two answers sound right and close the conversation, and a third one keeps it open.

## Two answers that close the door

The first is instant praise: *Of course you are! You scored last week!* He knows that goal deflected in off another kid's shin.

Tell him he's great when he feels otherwise, and he stops trusting your read on him.

The second is the therapist question. *What makes you say that?* He's eight, and he wants his dad in the front seat, not an interviewer.

## The answer that keeps him talking

Wait a beat, longer than feels comfortable.

Then try this: *Yeah. There are a few things you're still working on, and you're getting better at them. Want to talk about which ones?*

He says his left foot is bad. So you talk about his left foot for three minutes, and he decides he wants to work on it at practice next week.

For the rest of the drive, [what to say after a game](/team-parent/car-ride-home-what-parents-should-say/) and [the first 90 seconds](/drive-home/the-90-second-rule/) cover the quieter defaults.

He didn't need you to disagree. He needed you to take him seriously.
