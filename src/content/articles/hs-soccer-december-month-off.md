The school season ends in November. For a lot of families, a December club practice is already sitting in the group chat before the final whistle blows.

Our [season calendar](/season-calendar/hs-soccer-fall/) marks December as rest. That gap only exists if somebody writes it on the family calendar before the club fills it with winter sessions.

**What the pediatricians actually say.** The American Academy of Pediatrics updated its guidance on overuse, overtraining, and burnout in young athletes in 2024. Its number is bigger than two weeks.

The report advises at least 2 to 3 months a year away from any specific sport, split into one-month blocks if needed. It also asks for weekly rest.

That means 1 to 2 days off per week from competition and sport-specific training, plus one team per season. Check your fall against both.

**Why December is the block to take.** A kid who plays school soccer in the fall and club the rest of the year has very few natural gaps on the calendar. December is one of them.

The school season has just ended and winter break is coming, so a month away costs less here than anywhere else in the year. That banks one block. The other one or two can come later, when the club calendar has its own quiet stretch in summer or spring.

**Off soccer is not off the couch.** The AAP report says kids should focus on other activities or free play during the break, and that still counts as being active. Sledding and pickup basketball both count.

Our [off-season by age piece](/drive-there/what-off-season-actually-means/) breaks down what that looks like for a 12-year-old compared with a 16-year-old.

**Send the club one short note.** Do it before the school season ends, while the coach is still planning winter sessions and nobody has built a roster around your kid yet. Keep it to three lines.

Give the dates your kid will be off, say the break follows the AAP's guidance, and ask what to work on before sessions resume. If club and school overlap again next fall, our [shared week note](/team-parent/tell-the-club-which-nights-school-owns/) covers that conversation.

**Watch the restart, too.** A kid who dreads going back after a month away is telling you something worth hearing, even if the club is excited to see them. Our [burnout signs page](/body/burnout-signs/) lists what to watch.
