Your sister already texted the Thanksgiving plan: Thursday dinner at her house, kids table set. Then the team group chat drops a Thanksgiving tournament bracket with a Friday morning puck drop three states away.

The rink will not move. Dinner can.

Our [youth travel hockey calendar](/season-calendar/youth-travel-hockey-winter/) puts November under league games and, for many teams, a Thanksgiving tournament.

Same calendar tells families to plan Christmas travel around the holiday tournament, because the team will not plan the tournament around you. Thanksgiving follows that rule.

**Read the schedule before you book anything else.** Association packets, TeamSnap posts, and hotel blocks land on different weeks depending on your region and league.

Short version: your team's dates are the only dates that count.

Book early flights and you are guessing. Guessing tastes like airport pizza on Thursday night, then a missed Friday morning faceoff in another state.

**Three decisions, after the schedule is real.** Travel with the team. Move family dinner to Wednesday night or Sunday lunch.

Stay home for relatives and accept a sit-out weekend if your association allows it. Or split the household: one parent at the rink, one at the table, with the kid's preference named out loud before anyone gets mad in the parking lot.

None of those options is wrong. Pretending the bracket is soft until Wednesday night is the mistake that wrecks the weekend.

**Hotel and travel spend stack on top of association fees.** A tournament weekend is a real line item inside a youth hockey budget that already runs thousands of dollars across fees, ice, and gear.

[How much youth hockey costs](/drive-there/how-much-does-youth-hockey-cost/) walks the season total. Hand that page to a relative who asks why you cannot skip one weekend.

Going? Pull the [game-day packing list](/drive-there/hockey-game-day-packing-list/) the night before you roll.

Skates, sticks, and a cooler travel better when they are not packed at five in the morning in a panic.

**One calendar action this week.** Ask the team manager or coach for the Thanksgiving weekend plan in writing: host city, first puck drop, and whether a sit-out needs a formal note.

Put that answer on the shared family calendar next to the turkey plan. Then text your sister back with a real date for dinner.
