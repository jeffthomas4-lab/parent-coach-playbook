Picture a week where school practice starts Monday at 3:15, and the club still lists Tuesday, Thursday, and Sunday. Your 12-year-old is listed on the school roster and the club roster. Nobody has cut a night yet.

Both calendars are still full, and one note can replace the old one. The club keeps that calendar until a parent sends a new plan.

## Send the cap before you ask

Write the week yourself before you email anyone. Send it to the club coach, or to the director if the coach does not handle attendance. Do not open with a question about what works for the club.

Asking what works hands the whole calendar back to the program that already filled Tuesday, Thursday, and Sunday.

The note carries four facts. Name the school sport, the real start date, and the real end date. Then name the nights school owns, the nights club keeps, and one weekday with neither program on it.

Put the shared-day ban in that note as its own line. A day with both programs is how a middle school week blows past bedtime.

Use the dates from your school email. The block below is only a shape:

> School volleyball on this sample calendar starts October 6 and ends November 12. Practice runs Monday and Wednesday, 3:15 to 5:00.

> Club stays on Tuesday and on Sunday, while Thursday is off for both programs.

> We will not schedule school and club on the same day, and we need a reply by Friday if Tuesday has to move. Silence means we follow this plan.

Swap the sport, the dates, and the clock times for the ones printed in the school email before you send a word of this sample. A sample week is not your district start date.

## Say the same plan at home

Read the plan out loud the day you send it. An 11- to 14-year-old should not have to settle a double-booked night while standing in a parking lot. Using the sample week, it sounds like this:

> Monday and Wednesday belong to school, Tuesday and Sunday stay with club, and Thursday is empty on purpose so the week has a real off day.

> If a coach asks for a closed night, say the plan was already emailed and that questions go to a parent.

Write the nights you emailed where the adults will see them before anyone grabs keys. On the sample week, that line is school on Monday and Wednesday, club on Tuesday and Sunday, Thursday off, no same-day double.

The club does not need a speech about the family calendar. Your child does not need to pick a side between two adults. You already named the week.

## Keep one sport a day and one day off

The American Academy of Pediatrics' [2024 clinical report on overuse, overtraining, and burnout](https://publications.aap.org/pediatrics/article/153/2/e2023065129/196435/Overuse-Injuries-Overtraining-and-Burnout-in-Young) says young athletes should do no more than one sport in a day. It also says they should take at least one day each week off all organized sports.

Overlapping schedules should not erase that rest day. The same report says athletes should have two to three months off from each sport in a year.

Those lines are pediatric guidance from the AAP, not a diagnosis of your child and not a promise that any calendar prevents injury. They are the cap inside the email.

Before you agree to a second full week, read [one sport per season](/drive-home/the-case-for-one-sport-per-season/) for the case that one active roster is enough.

## Leave the off day actually off

Leave the off day empty of organized sport, including any hour a coach marks optional. A school open gym is organized sport, so it does not belong on the off day.

Free play in the yard does not break the cap. A whistle and a roster do.

And an off day a coach calls just-this-once is still club, so skip it unless you first move some other night in writing.

## If the club wants the old week

Ask one written question: which club dates are required if your child is going to stay on the next roster?

Until a director writes that a skills session, an extra scrimmage, or a come-if-you-can night is required, treat that hour as optional and skip it on a closed day.

If the written answer demands every current club practice on top of the new school practices, the cap does not fit. Drop a night, or drop a team.

Year-round club pressure is covered in the [early-specialization pressure guide](/team-parent/dealing-with-early-specialization-pressure/). Basketball families hitting an October tryout crash should read [school tryouts versus AAU](/drive-there/when-school-basketball-tryouts-and-aau-collide/), which solves a different week than this note.

You send the reply the same day the club writes back, from a parent email address, not from your child's phone. A 12-year-old does not negotiate roster rules in a group text.
