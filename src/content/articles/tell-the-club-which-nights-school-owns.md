Picture a week where school practice starts Monday at 3:15, and the club still lists Tuesday, Thursday, and Sunday. Your 12-year-old is on the school roster and the club roster. Nobody has decided which nights stay.

Both calendars are still full. A short note can replace that old week with one plan the club, you, and your child can all repeat. Until a parent sends it, the club will keep the calendar it already published.

## Write the week, then ask them to share it

Write the week you can live with before you email anyone. Send it to the club coach, or to the director if the coach does not handle attendance. Lead with the nights you already know, and ask them to confirm the club side or name a swap. An open "what works for you?" usually comes back as the full Tuesday, Thursday, and Sunday slate. A finished week, offered as a draft, gives them something specific to answer.

Put the school sport in the note, with the real start date and the real end date. Then name the nights school keeps, the nights you hope club keeps, and one weekday with neither program on it. Say that you do not want school and club on the same day. That double is how a middle school week slides past bedtime.

Use the dates from your school email. The block below is only a shape:

> School volleyball on this sample calendar starts October 6 and ends November 12. Practice runs Monday and Wednesday, 3:15 to 5:00.

> We would like club to stay on Tuesday and Sunday, with Thursday off for both programs.

> We are trying to keep school and club off the same day. If Tuesday has to move, could you reply by Friday so we can adjust the week together? If we do not hear back, we will follow this plan and check in.

Swap the sport, the dates, and the clock times for the ones printed in the school email before you send a word of this sample. A sample week is not your district start date.

## Say the same plan at home

Read the plan out loud the day you send it. An 11- to 14-year-old should not have to settle a double-booked night while standing in a parking lot. Using the sample week, it sounds like this:

> Monday and Wednesday are school. Tuesday and Sunday stay with club. Thursday is empty on purpose, so the week has a real off day.

> If a coach asks about a closed night, say the plan was already emailed and that a parent will answer.

Write the nights you emailed where the adults will see them before anyone grabs keys. On the sample week, that line is school on Monday and Wednesday, club on Tuesday and Sunday, Thursday off, and no same-day double.

The club does not need a speech about the family calendar. Your child does not need to choose between two adults. What helps is that all three of you can name the same nights.

## Keep one sport a day and one day off

The American Academy of Pediatrics' [2024 clinical report on overuse, overtraining, and burnout](https://publications.aap.org/pediatrics/article/153/2/e2023065129/196435/Overuse-Injuries-Overtraining-and-Burnout-in-Young) says young athletes should do no more than one sport in a day. It also says they should take at least one day each week off all organized sports.

A week with both school and club should not erase that rest day. The same report says athletes should have two to three months off from each sport in a year.

Those lines are pediatric guidance from the AAP, not a diagnosis of your child and not a promise that any calendar prevents injury. They are the reason the email asks for a lighter week.

Before you agree to a second full week, read [one sport per season](/drive-home/the-case-for-one-sport-per-season/) for the case that one active roster is enough.

## Leave the off day actually off

Leave the off day empty of organized sport, including any hour a coach marks optional. A school open gym counts as organized sport, so it does not belong on the off day. Free play in the yard is a different thing from a whistle and a roster.

If a coach asks for that off day just this once, answer in writing. You can say yes if some other night comes off the week first, so the rest day is still there.

## If the club needs more nights

Ask one written question: which club dates are required if your child is going to stay on the next roster?

Until a director writes that a skills session, an extra scrimmage, or a come-if-you-can night is required, treat that hour as optional and skip it on a closed day.

If the written answer asks for every current club practice on top of the new school practices, the week you wrote will not hold. Look together for one night to drop, or for one team to pause, so your child can stay on a roster that still fits.

Year-round club pressure is covered in the [early-specialization pressure guide](/team-parent/dealing-with-early-specialization-pressure/). Basketball families hitting an October tryout crash should read [school tryouts versus AAU](/drive-there/when-school-basketball-tryouts-and-aau-collide/), which solves a different week than this note.

Reply the same day the club writes back, from a parent email address, not from your child's phone. A 12-year-old should not have to work out roster rules in a group text.

## What you are each agreeing to

You are agreeing to send a clear week and to answer from a parent email. The club is agreeing to the nights that stay, and to a note if a required date has to move. Your child is agreeing to show up for those nights and to send coach questions back to you.

School keeps its practices. Club keeps a real place on the calendar, and a roster it can count on. The off day stays off, which is what lets the week hold. They know who is coming, and you know which bag goes in the car.
