October club volleyball emails arrive with a familiar pitch: skill clinic, prep session, open gym. Pay now, get touches before tryouts, and the tone implies you are already late if you skip it.

Our [club volleyball 14s calendar](/season-calendar/club-volleyball-14s/) puts those pre-tryout skill clinics in October. November is the month marked for USAV-regulated club tryouts, team selections, and first practices.

That gap is the money question. Clinics are not the same event as tryouts, even when the same coaches run both in the same gym.

**Clinics and tryouts are different products.** A tryout is the evaluation window that feeds roster offers and season contracts. A pre-tryout clinic is paid gym time: skill stations, scrimmage reps, a chance to meet staff, and a read on whether the club culture fits your family.

Clubs have a right to charge a tryout fee. Separate clinic nights show up on the same registration page and still mean something else.

**What Region guidance actually says.** Rules are Region-specific. USA Volleyball Regions publish their own junior tryout calendars and commitment timing, the way [SCSN posts division tryout open dates and Letter of Commitment windows for 2026-27](https://usavolleyball.org/scsn/scsn-junior-girls/). Pull your Region page before you treat one club email as national law.

The Carolina Region Athlete and Club Bill of Rights for Tryouts draws a clean line parents can use even when their Region writes different words. Open gym sessions or instructional clinics are usually not considered part of the tryout process, and most clubs do not require athletes to attend those pre-tryout events in order to attend tryouts.

Carolina calls that Bill of Rights consumer guidance clubs are encouraged to adopt, not a hard statute with automatic sanctions. The consumer framing still helps: you are buying a product, so ask what you are buying.

Arizona Region open-house rules go further in print. During the Open House period a club may run limited sessions and talk about fit, but it may not require you to attend more than one Open House at that club, may not make offers or collect commitment down payments in that window, and may not ask for a commitment before tryouts.

**Club copy you will see.** Club FAQs label prep clinics "not required" even while calling them "highly recommended." That split is the honest version. Recommended means useful for some families. Required means you cannot get a fair tryout look without the fee.

If a registration page or coach text flips "recommended" into "you have to be here to be evaluated," ask for the Region rule that says so. Ask in writing.

**When a clinic is worth the fee.** Go if your kid has never set foot in that gym and you want one low-stakes look before you pay a tryout fee. Go if you are comparing two clubs and need to see how coaches teach, how crowded the floor gets, and whether the age group feels like a real match.

Skip the clinic if the only goal is buying a roster look. The tryout still happens. Our [youth volleyball tryouts primer](/drive-there/youth-volleyball-tryouts-what-to-expect/) already covers what coaches watch once that evaluation starts: pass quality, communication, coachability, and effort under live ball.

**The three-club stack.** Families shopping club volleyball in October register for clinics at Club A, Club B, and Club C, then pay three tryout fees in November. None of those dollars is the season deposit yet.

That stack is how a cheap clinic night turns into real money before anyone has an offer. Our [club volleyball overview](/drive-there/club-volleyball-what-parents-need-to-know/) and [cost breakdown](/drive-there/youth-volleyball-cost-breakdown/) already warn that the club fee is only the first line. Clinics belong on the same honest spreadsheet.

Pick one clinic. Bank every leftover dollar for the November tryout fees you still plan to pay at the clubs that actually made your short list.

**Sales-pitch signals.** Treat these as stop signs, not as proof the program is bad at volleyball.

A club that says clinic attendance is mandatory for a fair look while its own FAQ or your Region page says clinics are optional. A club that pushes a verbal commitment or a hold fee before the published tryout window. A club that bundles clinic payment as if it were the tryout fee, then still charges a separate tryout fee without saying so up front.

Pressure to commit before the club's announced commitment date is the pattern Carolina's Bill of Rights calls out as unfair to families who want to try out elsewhere. Offers belong to the tryout process your Region defines, not to the October marketing blast.

**School ball still matters in October.** Older kids may still be finishing school volleyball while club clinics open. Protect eligibility and sleep before you add a third weeknight clinic for a club you have not even decided to try out for.

**The one question to ask this week.** Ask each club, in writing: Is the clinic required to attend your tryout, or optional? What is the tryout fee on top of the clinic fee? When does your Region allow offers and commitments?

Put the answers next to your calendar for November tryouts, then spend only on the clinics that change a real decision.
