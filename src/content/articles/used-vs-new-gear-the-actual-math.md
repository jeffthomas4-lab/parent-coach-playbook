A used item wins only after it clears fit, safety, and league rules. A low price cannot repair an expired certification, unknown impact history, outgrown size, or bat that is illegal in the child's division.

Use the [used-gear safety rules](/drive-there/used-gear-rules/) first. This page handles the budget after an item is eligible for consideration.

## Calculate cost per usable season

Use one line for each option:

**Purchase price + required inspection or reconditioning + replacement parts + shipping - realistic resale value = net cost**

Then divide net cost by the number of seasons the child can reasonably use the item before growth, wear, rule changes, or a manufacturer limit ends its use.

Do not count a hoped-for second season if the fit is already marginal. Do not assign resale value to personalized, worn, expired, or hard-to-verify equipment.

This method does not need a universal discount percentage. Record the actual local used price and the actual current new price on the same day.

## Put safety and legality ahead of the equation

Some costs are gates, not tradeoffs. If a helmet's required certification cannot be verified, its history is unknown, or the manufacturer does not permit the needed recertification, leave it out of the comparison.

NOCSAE's [recertification guidance](https://nocsae.org/general-recertification-requirements-and-recommendations/) says helmet intervals depend on manufacturer requirements. A used football or lacrosse helmet that can be professionally inspected and recertified is different from an untraceable marketplace helmet.

CPSC bicycle-helmet guidance warns that impact damage may be invisible and directs replacement after such an impact. Unknown crash history makes a used bicycle helmet a poor candidate regardless of price.

For a bat, verify the current league mark and approved status before comparing models. USA Baseball explains that non-wood bats used under USABat rules need a permanent [USABat certification mark](https://www.usabaseball.com/bats/about), and a decertified model is no longer approved in adopting leagues.

## Compare the categories that fit the method

Used bags, practice clothing, many cleats, and simple training accessories often have easy-to-see condition and no certification cycle. Check fit, seams, soles, fasteners, mold, and the cost of any replacement part.

New can win when the child will use several seasons, a warranty has real value, or the used option needs service and shipping. Used can win when fit is confirmed, condition is visible, and a growing child is likely to outgrow the item before wearing it out.

For baseball, resolve the league mark with the [USA versus USSSA bat guide](/game/usa-vs-usssa-bats/) before doing the math. For football, compare the program-provided list with the [youth football equipment guide](/drive-there/youth-football-equipment-guide/) before assuming the family needs to buy every item.

Write the net cost and usable seasons beside each option. The smaller number is the better value only when both choices have already passed the same safety, fit, and rules screen.
