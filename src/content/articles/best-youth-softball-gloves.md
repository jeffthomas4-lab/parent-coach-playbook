## How do you choose the right youth softball glove?

Start here before you look at a single brand name.

**Quick fit checklist:**

- Measure your child's hand from the base of the palm to the tip of the middle finger
- Match that measurement to the age-based size chart below
- Confirm left-hand or right-hand thrower (the glove goes on the *opposite* hand from the throwing hand)
- Run the one-hand close test: your child should be able to close the glove around a ball using one hand alone; if they can't, the glove is too stiff or too large
- Check the wrist strap — it should adjust snugly without pinching

Softball gloves have a wider, deeper pocket than baseball gloves because the softball is larger and heavier. A baseball-style shallow pocket causes the ball to sit awkwardly or pop out on contact. If your child has been using a baseball glove for softball, this is worth fixing.

Webbing matters more than most parents realize. Open-web patterns (I-web, H-web) help infielders transfer the ball quickly. Closed webs are standard for pitchers, who need to hide their grip. For players under 10 who rotate positions, a basic modified trap or H-web works fine for everything.

**Synthetic vs. leather at a glance:**

Synthetic gloves are lighter, softer, and ready to use the day you buy them. They cost less and wear out faster, but for a 7- or 8-year-old who will outgrow the glove in one season, that trade-off is completely reasonable. Leather gloves last longer and hold their shape better, but they require a break-in period and more hand strength to close. Most youth players benefit from softer, game-ready gloves rather than stiff pro-leather models, precisely because younger players often lack the grip strength to close a rigid glove consistently.

**Where to buy:**

In-store sizing at a sporting goods retailer like Dick's Sporting Goods or Academy Sports is worth the trip for first-time buyers. You can run the one-hand close test on the spot. Online purchasing works well for repeat buyers who already know their child's size, and it opens up more brand options. Always check the return policy before buying online, especially for younger players whose sizing can be tricky to predict.

**Pro Tip:** *Never buy a glove "to grow into." A glove that is one inch too large reduces control, slows transfers, and teaches a child to squeeze with two hands out of necessity. Buy the right size for this season and plan to replace it in 12–18 months.*

## What size glove does your child need for softball?

The table below maps age bands to recommended glove sizes. These are starting points; always confirm with the one-hand close test.

| Age / Level | Recommended Glove Size | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 6U / Tee Ball | 9"–10" | Soft synthetic, utility style |
| 8U | 10"–10.5" | Utility glove, no position-specific needed |
| 10U | 10.5"–11" | Begin considering pocket depth for the larger ball |
| 12U | Infield: 11"–11.5", Outfield: 11"–11.5" | Position-specific sizing starts to matter |
| 14U and up | Infield: 11.5"–12", Outfield: 12"–13" | Position-specific sizing becomes relevant |

*Sources: [Rawlings sizing chart](https://www.rawlings.com/sizing-gloves.html); Legion Report softball glove size guide*

**How to measure:**

1. Use a soft tape measure or a piece of string
2. Measure from the base of the palm (just above the wrist) to the tip of the middle finger
3. Compare to the chart above and round to the nearest half-inch
4. Run the one-hand close test with the glove on the child's non-throwing hand

**A quick example:** A 9-year-old right-hand thrower (glove on the left hand) with a 6" hand measurement fits comfortably in a 10"–10.5" utility glove. She plays multiple positions in 8U rec ball, so a utility-style glove with a modified trap web is the right call.

**Ball size matters for pocket depth.** [Regulation softballs are 12 inches for players aged 10 and older](https://www.academy.com/expert-advice/baseball-softball-glove-differences); younger players typically use an 11-inch ball. A glove sized for an 11" ball will have a shallower pocket than one designed for the 12" ball, so as your child moves into 10U and beyond, confirm the glove's pocket is designed for the larger ball.

## Leather vs. synthetic: which material fits your child right now?

The honest answer depends on age, playing level, and how long you expect the glove to last.

**Synthetic gloves** are lighter, softer, and ready to use immediately. They cost less, which matters when you're buying for a 7-year-old who will outgrow the glove in a single season. The trade-off is durability: synthetic materials wear faster, and a heavily used glove may not survive two full seasons.

**Leather gloves** last longer and hold their shape better over time. A well-maintained leather glove can carry a player through two to three seasons if the sizing holds. The catch is break-in. Stiff leather requires consistent work to soften, and younger players often lack the hand strength to close a new leather glove reliably. For players under 10, that stiffness actively gets in the way of learning to field.

## How do you break in and care for a youth softball glove?

A new glove that's too stiff is one of the most common reasons young players struggle to field cleanly. Breaking it in correctly takes a few days of intentional effort, not a microwave or an oven.

**Step-by-step break-in process:**

1. Apply a small amount of glove conditioner (Rawlings Glovolium or a comparable product) to the palm and pocket. Less is more; too much conditioner softens the leather excessively and shortens the glove's life.
2. Place a softball in the pocket and fold the glove closed around it. Secure it with a rubber band or glove wrap overnight.
3. The next day, have your child wear the glove and open and close it repeatedly by hand for 10–15 minutes. This works the hinge at the palm.
4. Play catch at moderate intensity for 20–30 minutes. Real catches do more than any tool or technique.
5. Repeat steps 2–4 for three to five days. By day five, most youth gloves are noticeably more pliable.

**Dos and don'ts for care:**

- **Do** store the glove with a ball in the pocket to maintain the shape
- **Do** wipe dirt off after each use with a dry cloth
- **Do** apply conditioner once or twice per season, not after every game
- **Don't** leave the glove in a hot car or direct sunlight; heat dries out leather and warps synthetic materials
- **Don't** soak the glove in water to speed up break-in; this weakens the lacing and the leather
- **Do** check the lacing at the start of each season and replace any frayed or broken laces before they fail mid-game

**How do you know the glove is properly broken in?** The pocket should hold its shape when you place a ball in it without your hand inside. The glove should close smoothly with one hand and spring back open when released. If it still feels like a board, give it another week of catch.

**Pro Tip:** *For a quick game-day softening fix, apply a small amount of conditioner to the palm and pocket the night before, then work the glove by hand for five minutes. This won't replace a full break-in, but it takes the edge off a stiff glove before a big game.*

## Is your child's glove still the right fit?

Run this checklist at the start of each season and again mid-season if your child has had a growth spurt.

**Field fit checklist:**

- **One-hand close test:** Can your child close the glove around a ball using one hand? If not, the glove is too large or too stiff.
- **Finger-stall fit:** Fingers should reach the tips of the stalls without bunching. A half-inch of space at the fingertips is fine; more than that means the glove is too large.
- **Wrist strap:** Should fasten snugly without cutting into the wrist. If the strap is maxed out, the hand opening may be too small.
- **Transfer speed:** Have your child catch a soft toss and transfer to the throwing hand. If the transfer is slow or awkward, the pocket may be too deep or the glove too heavy.
- **Lacing integrity:** Check for fraying or broken laces, especially at the web and heel.

**When to replace:**

Youth softball gloves typically need replacement every 1–2 seasons for players aged 7–12, primarily because of hand growth rather than wear. Signs it's time for a new glove: the finger stalls are visibly short, the wrist strap is at maximum, or the pocket has collapsed and won't hold its shape.

**When to upgrade to a position-specific glove:**

Around age 12–13, when a player settles into a primary position and begins playing at a competitive level, a position-specific glove starts to make sense. An infielder benefits from a shorter glove with an open web for faster transfers. An outfielder needs a longer glove with a deeper pocket. Until that point, a quality utility glove serves every position well.

## A note from one parent-coach to another

Here's something worth saying plainly: the glove does not make the player. The right glove, sized correctly, gets out of the way and lets your child focus on the game. The wrong glove, even an expensive one, becomes a distraction every time a ball pops out of a pocket that's too shallow or a child squeezes with two hands because one isn't strong enough to close a stiff leather glove.

What I see most often is parents buying up. They want to give their child every advantage, and a premium glove feels like an investment in that. But a 9-year-old in a $200 stiff leather glove is not better equipped than a 9-year-old in an $60 soft synthetic that fits perfectly. The second child will catch more balls, build more confidence, and actually enjoy fielding.

The other thing worth naming: confidence at the field starts with gear that works. When a child can close their glove cleanly, transfer the ball quickly, and not think about their equipment, they play freely. That freedom is what youth sports should feel like.
