An eight-year-old doesn't need cleats on the first day of practice. Plenty of kids wear them because a parent assumed the league required them. Watch first.

## The slip test

Indoor court or hard-packed dirt: flat shoes work. Grass or turf where your kid slips on cuts and starts: cleats earn their keep.

Let them play the first few weeks in running shoes, and watch what happens on the cuts. If they slip, buy cleats. If they don't, you kept the money.

Shin guards are different: they're the one piece of soccer gear that isn't optional at any age. The [IFAB Laws of the Game](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/the-players-equipment/) list shinguards, covered by the socks, as compulsory equipment for every player.

## What to buy and what to spend

Go molded firm-ground: rubber studs, no metal. Molded studs work on grass, turf, and dry fields, which covers nearly every field an eight-year-old plays on.

Skip soft-ground cleats. Those carry the longer screw-in studs built for mud, and they're miserable on a firm field. Check your league's rules before you buy anything with metal or screw-in studs.

Fit beats brand. Go half a size up for growing room, have them try the pair on in soccer socks, and walk the store aisle. If they say the cleats are tight, they're too tight.

Plan on $40 to $60 at this age. In October 2026, kids' club-level firm-ground models from Adidas and Nike listed at about $45 to $55. Anything above that range at eight is paying for the logo, and the [soccer gear guide](/what-to-buy/soccer/) has specific picks by age.

## The growth problem

They'll outgrow them in one season. Not because the cleats are bad. Because they're eight, and an eight-year-old's feet grow faster than the cleat budget does.

That's normal, and it's not your fault. When it happens in October, [the mid-season outgrow plan](/drive-there/when-your-kid-outgrows-cleats-mid-season/) walks through it, and [cleat care](/team-parent/cleat-care-that-triples-the-lifespan/) keeps a pair alive long enough to hand down.

Get what fits. Ignore the logo. Their foot doesn't care what swoosh you paid for.
