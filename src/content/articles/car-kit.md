The trunk kit handles forgotten socks and wet clothes. It is not a first-aid kit, a pantry, or a second equipment bag.

Small wins here. If the bin takes over the trunk, the bin has lost the plot.

## Pack the boring backups

Use a small lidded bin or zip bag. Start with the items that work across sports:

- two pairs of plain athletic socks
- one towel and two sealable bags for wet gear
- one warm layer and one rain layer that still fit
- hair ties, period products, and sunscreen if your family uses them
- a spare team-approved mouthguard or other personal item only when the program permits it

Check the product label before leaving anything in a vehicle. If the maker gives a storage-temperature limit or says not to store it in a car, keep it in the player's bag instead.

## Leave treatment to the right person

Do not turn the trunk bin into an athletic training room. Leave pain relievers, loose medical supplies, and improvised braces or wraps out of the general kit.

If a player is hurt, follow the team's emergency plan and hand the decision to the parent, coach, athletic trainer, or clinician responsible for that player. Spare tape is not an injury plan.

The [season essentials guide](/what-to-buy/season-essentials/) covers product categories after your family decides what it uses. Keep this kit focused on clean backups.

## Keep food and water in a separate plan

Food, filled bottles, and coolers need their own game-day check. The guide to [what to keep in the car all season](/drive-there/what-to-keep-in-the-car/) owns that packing problem.

Separating the two jobs also makes cleanup obvious. Wet gear comes out after the event.

Food comes out the same day. The dry backup bin stays.

For a longer trip, use the [tournament weekend packing list](/drive-there/tournament-weekend-packing-list/) instead of trying to make one trunk bin cover a hotel stay.

Open the kit before the first practice. Remove expired, leaking, damaged, or outgrown items, wash the towel and layers, and replace what the family used.

Then close the lid. The next forgotten-socks problem should take ten seconds.
