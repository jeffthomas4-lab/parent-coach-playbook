A soccer center circle isn't drawn freehand. IFAB sets its radius at exactly 9.15 meters, 10 yards, measured out from the center mark on the halfway line. A stake, a length of string, and a can of field paint get you there faster than any tape-and-guess method.

## Peg the center, then swing the string

Find the halfway line first. It splits the field in half and connects the midpoints of both touchlines.

Drive a stake at the exact center of that line and tie one end of your string to it. Cut the string to 10 yards, or 9.15 meters if you're working metric, and knot a paint marker or a second stake to the free end.

Keep the string taut and walk the full circle around the center stake, marking as you go. Slack in the line turns your circle into an oval, and nobody notices until the referee steps off the kickoff distance and it's wrong.

## The 10 yards isn't a rounding choice

IFAB's Law 8.1 requires every opponent to stay at least 10 yards from the ball at kickoff, and the center circle is the visible line that keeps that distance honest. Players and referees read it on sight instead of pacing off ten steps every restart.

Draw the circle short and you've handed the kicking team less room than the rule gives them. Draw it long and the players line up outside a boundary that doesn't mean anything.

## Check it before the first game

Measure across the circle at its widest point once you're done. Sixty feet, or 20 yards, corner to corner confirms the radius held steady the whole way around.

A center circle only has to be marked once a season if the paint holds. Touch it up the same way you started: stake in the center, string cut to 10 yards, walk it around.
