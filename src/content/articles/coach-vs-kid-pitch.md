In coach pitch, a parent or volunteer pitches to the batter. In kid pitch, also called player pitch, a child takes the mound and throws to the other team. Most 5 to 8 year olds belong in coach pitch or machine pitch, because more contact means more confidence.

Kid pitch comes later, and the call turns on four things:

- **Throwing control:** can your kid throw strikes from the league's mound distance?
- **Emotional readiness:** can they handle a walk, a wild pitch, or a strikeout without shutting down?
- **League rules:** what does your board allow for your kid's age division?
- **Tournament format:** does your travel or tournament schedule require kid pitch?

***

## Key Takeaways

Coach pitch builds contact and confidence. Kid pitch builds pitching and the full game once a kid is ready for it.

| Point | Details |
|---|---|

## How coach pitch and kid pitch compare across what parents care about

| Dimension | Coach Pitch | Kid (Player) Pitch |
|---|---|---|
| Best for | Contact reps, hitting confidence, fundamentals | Pitching mechanics, full-game rules |
| Typical age | Younger divisions, roughly 5 to 8 | 8U in some leagues, standard by 9U and 10U |
| Pitching distance | Set by your league | 40 feet in USSSA 8U; longer as kids age |
| Stealing | Usually limited | Allowed with limits that vary by organization |
| Walks and strikeouts | Few or no walks; pitch limits per at-bat | Walks and strikeouts in play; pitch counts tracked |
| Coach's role | Pitches, then stays out of the play | Coaches from the dugout |
| Balls in play | Many | Fewer early in the season |
| Pace | Faster | Slower with young pitchers |

**Pro Tip:** *Some leagues use hybrid rules: the kid pitches until four balls, then the coach finishes the at-bat. Ask your board about this before the season.*

Machine pitch is worth a look when adult pitching varies across the league. A steady machine strike beats a wild adult arm for a 7-year-old learning to hit.

## What coach pitch actually looks like on game day

The coach throws pitches meant to be hit. Most leagues cap pitches per at-bat.

The [Little League Coach Pitch program](https://www.littleleague.org/play-little-league/coach-pitch/) gives volunteer managers practice plans and guidance. Its whole idea is that early baseball should build a kid's confidence, not drain it.

**Logistics for volunteer coaches:**

- Pitch from the line or circle your league marks.
- Step aside right after the pitch, because you are not a fielder. In the Ely BC 8U rules, a coach who fields a batted ball makes the batter out.
- Throw a flat, hittable pitch. Some leagues allow kneeling and some require a standing overhand throw, so check.
- Know what happens when a batted ball hits you. [Perfect Game's tournament rules](https://www.perfectgame.org/Events/tournaments/rules.aspx?event=136538) spell out their version, and your league may differ.

**Practice drills that carry over:**

- Soft toss from the side to build timing.
- Front toss from a knee to copy a coach's flat pitch.
- Station batting so every player gets reps.

Short, station-based practices fit this age. Forty-five focused minutes beat two hours of kids standing around in the outfield waiting for a ball.

## What kid pitch changes about the game

Now a child takes the mound. Walks happen, strikeouts happen, the pace slows, and the stakes feel higher for pitcher and batter alike.

**Rule changes you'll see:**

- A longer mound distance as kids move up divisions.
- Stealing, with timing rules that vary by organization.
- Walks that can stack into long innings while young pitchers find the zone.
- Pitch counts, with daily limits and rest days set by the governing body.

The [USSSA 8U kid-pitch rule highlights](https://emaildesigner.usssa.com/email-template/8u-kid-pitch-sent-1-12/) give one concrete example. USSSA lists 60-foot base paths, a 40-foot pitching distance, a 75-minute time limit, and a 7-run limit per inning. Treat those as a reference point, since other organizations set their own.

Hybrid rules show up at 8U. In the [sample league rules from Ely BC](https://elybc.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/8U-Kid-Pitch-Rules.pdf), there are no walks from a kid pitcher, and at four balls the coach comes in to pitch to that hitter with the strike count carried over.

**What kid pitch asks of players:**

- Young pitchers feel real pressure, and some need another season of coach pitch first.
- Batters see more strikeouts, which stings for kids who crushed coach pitch last year.
- Fielders get fewer chances early, and the game opens up as pitchers improve.

## How governing bodies and local leagues decide which format to use

No national rule says 8-year-olds play coach pitch. Local boards decide, and 8U can be coach pitch, kid pitch, or a hybrid.

Boards weigh how many coaches can pitch strikes, which fields they have, and what tournaments require. A league that feeds a tournament circuit may move kids to player pitch earlier than a rec league built for fun.

Many leagues also let a player move up or down a division by local option. A ready 7-year-old might pitch in 8U, while an 8-year-old who isn't ready stays in coach pitch another year.

**Ask your league before the season:**

- The exact division your kid is registered in.
- The pitching distance used in games.
- Whether hybrid rules apply, and after how many balls.
- When baserunners can steal.
- Whether tournaments use a different format than the regular season.

**Pro Tip:** *Ask about tournaments by name. A kid who sees coach pitch all season and kid pitch for the first time in a tournament is set up for a rough weekend.*

## How each format affects your child's development

Both formats can produce confident players. They build different things.

**Coach pitch: what it builds and where it falls short**

More contact means more fielding, more running, and more moments of success at the plate. At 6 or 7, that's the point.

Kids in coach pitch don't learn to pitch or to read a live arm. The jump to kid pitch can feel abrupt.

**Kid pitch: what it builds and where it gets hard**

Player pitch teaches the full game. Pitchers learn to compete, fielders learn to stay ready, and batters learn to lay off bad pitches.

The hard part is discouragement. A young pitcher who walks three in a row is struggling in front of everyone, and that is a lot for an 8-year-old to carry.

**Pro Tip:** *If coach pitching quality swings from team to team, propose machine pitch to the board. In one [community discussion among coaches](https://www.baseball-fever.com/forum/general-baseball/baseball-101-coaching-fundamentals/87592-7u-pitching-machine-kid-or-coach-pitch), coaches debate exactly this trade-off for 7U.*

**Pro Tip:** *For a kid on the edge, one more season of coach pitch rarely hurts, and confidence built at 8 carries into 10.*

## A practical checklist for deciding when your child is ready

Age is only part of it. Watch what your kid can do and how they handle the bad moments.

**Readiness signals:**

1. They throw strikes from the league's distance in practice most of the time.
2. They bounce back after a bad pitch, a strikeout, or an error.
3. They've hit against live pitching in practice.
4. They *want* to pitch.
5. Their coach likes what they see in the mechanics.

**Early-season evaluation:**

1. Watch 10 practice pitches at game distance and count the strikes.
2. Ask the coach which distance the league uses in games and tournaments.
3. Confirm whether hybrid rules apply and how the count carries over.
4. Ask about steal rules and whether the team practices baserunning.
5. Check the tournament schedule, and get live-pitch reps before the first one.

**A staged transition timeline:**

- **Off-season (October to February):** work on mechanics with a [60-minute practice blueprint](/team-parent/60-minute-practice-blueprint/).
- **Pre-season (March):** add live at-bats in scrimmages and track accuracy, not results.
- **First half (April to May):** use hybrid rules if your league has them.
- **Second half (June):** kids finding the zone go full kid pitch, and the rest stay hybrid or return to coach pitch next year without shame.

Our [10-minute parent meeting script](/team-parent/10-minute-parent-meeting-script/) helps you explain the plan to parents before confusion builds.

## What this decision really comes down to

Leagues choose differently, and most choices are reasonable. A league with ten coaches who throw strikes has options a league with three doesn't.

How well a format is run matters more than its label. A coach who pitches flat, hittable balls and stays out of the way gives kids a good season, and so does a kid-pitch program that teaches real mechanics.

An extra season of coach pitch is not a failure. A kid who builds a swing and a love of the game first is in no hurry, and plenty of late movers catch up.
