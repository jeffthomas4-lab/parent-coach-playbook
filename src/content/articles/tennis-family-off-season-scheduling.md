Junior tennis runs on tournaments entered one at a time, all twelve months of the year. Nothing on the USTA calendar tells a family when to stop. The closest thing to a natural break sits in September, right after the biggest national championships wrap and before the next tournament block opens.

**Why September is the window.** USTA junior events run from Level 7 local tournaments up through Level 1 National Championships, with the marquee national event on hard courts in early August. After that, players and coaches are both spent, and October brings local tournaments and rebuild work back into the calendar. September sits in the gap, and that gap is the only break anyone gets.

**What happens without one.** Without a deliberate block, September fills itself: a coach schedules lessons, a local tournament opens registration, a sibling's fall sport needs rides on the same weekends. The two weeks that could have been a break turn into another version of August.

**What to actually do.** Put two to three tournament-free weeks on the family calendar before October's rebuild tournaments open, not after. Tell the coach it's happening, so any planned technical work, a serve change, a grip adjustment, gets built around the break instead of competing with it. It just needs a date on the shared calendar, blocked before anyone else claims it.

A kid who plays tournament tennis every week of the year burns out faster than one who gets a real reset in the middle of it. September is the one month the calendar hands a tennis family for free. Use it before something else takes it.
