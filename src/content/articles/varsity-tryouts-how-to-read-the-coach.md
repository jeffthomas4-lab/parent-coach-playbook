Varsity tryouts turn ordinary coach behavior into a family code-breaking exercise.

The coach wrote something down. The coach did not write something down. The coach talked to one kid twice and another kid once.

None of that is a reliable roster forecast.

Read what the coach makes clear instead.

## Watch what gets taught and repeated

Notice whether the coach explains the drill, names the standard, and corrects the same mistake consistently. That tells your kid what the program values.

One coach may stop a session to fix spacing. Another may keep the drill moving and address it later. Different methods do not automatically mean good or bad.

The useful question is whether your teenager understands what is being asked and gets a fair chance to show it.

Ask after the session: *What did the coach repeat today?* The answer is better evidence than *Did the coach look happy when you played?*

## Separate program fit from roster prediction

Look at the ordinary parts: start time, transitions, water breaks, equipment, feedback, and how players are addressed. Could your kid work in that environment for a season?

Do not turn one sharp correction into a personality diagnosis. Do pay attention to a repeated pattern that your kid can describe with specifics.

If the school offers a parent or player meeting, use it to confirm communication rules, attendance expectations, and how questions are handled. Our [preseason meeting guide](/drive-there/pre-season-meeting-with-the-new-coach/) gives you the questions that belong there.

## Use the result to choose the next question

If your kid makes the team, ask what they learned about the standard and what the first week requires. The tryout is over. They do not need to keep auditioning at dinner.

If they do not make it, let the first reaction be about the disappointment. The [24-hour rule after a cut](/drive-home/after-the-cut-the-24-hour-rule/) keeps the first night free of emails, appeals, and emergency training plans.

Later, your teenager can ask whether feedback is available and what the next opportunity is. Some programs provide individual notes and some do not, so do not promise an answer the coach has not offered.

While the roster is still pending, use [the post-tryout waiting plan](/drive-there/the-post-tryouts-wait/) instead of reading every hallway interaction as news. A coach's glance is not a roster. The published roster is.
