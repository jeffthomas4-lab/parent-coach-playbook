Fall ball registration opens in September, right after all-stars wraps and the fields go quiet. The season runs six to eight weeks and is done before Halloween.

**When it happens.** August sits empty on most Little League calendars, so fall ball is what fills the gap instead of a clean break. Nobody's fighting over cage time in September the way spring does with everything else.

**What's different on the field.** Most leagues run free substitution: every kid rotates through every position across a game instead of getting locked into one spot for two months.

Pitch counts don't relax, though. Little League's daily caps, 85 pitches at ages 11-12 and 95 at 13-16, apply in fall ball exactly like they do in April. An arm doesn't know the difference between a September Tuesday and a May Saturday.

**What's gone.** No opening day parade, no picture day, no playoffs, no all-star tryout at the end of it. Some leagues skip trophies entirely.

None of that is a downgrade. Fall ball exists to strip out the postseason pressure spring carries, so losing the pageantry is the point.

**What it costs.** Registration runs well under spring, since the fee doesn't carry a tournament budget or a banquet, and most leagues reuse the spring uniform. The actual number swings more by league than spring fees do. Check your own league's page instead of trusting a figure from anywhere else.

**Is it worth it.** For a kid who wants more baseball and has room on the September calendar, yes: extra at-bats, a new position, no roster cut to worry about. For a kid also starting flag football or fall soccer, the calendar math gets tight before the value does.

Skipping it costs nothing long-term. The kid who plays every fall at 9 is not the kid who starts at shortstop at 15. That gets built over years, not extra Saturdays.

Fall ball helps. Not much, and not in a way that shows up at fifteen, but it helps. The question worth asking in September is whether your kid is the one asking for it.
