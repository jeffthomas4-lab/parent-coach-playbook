Pick the throwing drill by the problem you're fixing. Use quick catch to warm up, one-knee throws for arm slot, target throws for accuracy, and crow-hop throws for footwork. Three or four drills beat twenty minutes of one.

Know the safety baseline first. [USA Baseball and MLB's Pitch Smart program](https://www.usabaseball.com/news/mlb-usa-baseball-announce-updates-to-pitch-smart-program-251090688) sets pitch counts and rest days by age, and the [American Sports Medicine Institute guidance shared by Washington University](https://www.ortho.wustl.edu/mm/files/Patient%20Education/YACInjuryPreventionFlyer-2.pdf) calls for four months a year with no overhead throwing at all.

- **Quick catch:** warm-up and rhythm
- **Target throws:** accuracy and release
- **One-knee drill:** arm slot, with the legs taken out
- **Crow-hop throws:** footwork and distance
- **Split-step throws:** lower body into the throw
- **Bullseye games:** accuracy for younger kids

> **Safety flag:** a [PubMed review on youth pitchers](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18681352/) points to overuse as the big driver of arm injuries. Rest days matter as much as reps.

## Eight Baseball Throwing Drills You Can Run This Week

Every drill below fits inside one practice. Pick three or four based on what your team needs that day.

**Quick catch.** Pair kids 20 to 40 feet apart, closer for the youngest, for 15 to 20 easy throws. Cue "glove up" and "step to your partner."

**One-knee throws.** The thrower kneels on the throwing-side knee and throws 10 reps to a partner 15 to 20 feet away. With the legs out of it, you can see the arm path clearly. Cue "lead with your elbow."

**Accuracy target throws.** Set a [bucket](/go/baseball-ball-bucket-with-lid/), a [cone](/go/agility-cones-set/), or a [partner's glove](/go/baseball-glove-youth/) 20 to 30 feet away. Each kid gets 10 throws, and you write down the hits. Going from 4 of 10 to 7 of 10 over a month is a win a kid can see.

**Crow-hop throws.** A small skip-step before the throw moves energy from the legs into the arm. Start short and add distance slowly over weeks. Cue "gather, hop, throw on a line."

The [ASMI Interval Throwing Program](https://asmi.org/wp-content/uploads/Interval_Throwing_Program_for_Baseball_Pitchers-2019.pdf) is a different tool, and parents ask about it often enough that it's worth a clear note here. It's a return-to-throwing plan for injured players, run with a doctor's clearance.

**Split-step throws.** Roll a ball to an infielder, who fields it, lands on both feet, and throws 30 feet to a target. Ten to 15 reps is plenty.

**Wall bounce-back.** Solo driveway work: throw against a wall or rebounder from 15 feet, catch it, and repeat 20 to 30 times.

**Partner mirror drill.** Two kids face each other and copy each other's arm circle and follow-through without a ball. Five slow reps each builds body awareness.

**Bullseye game.** Paint rings or lay a hula hoop down and award points for the center. Younger kids love it.

**Pro Tip:** *Film two or three throws per kid each week with a [phone tripod](/go/phone-tripod/). Clips show change early.*

## Warm-Up and Arm-Care Checklist for Practice

Five minutes of movement comes before the first throw. Run arm circles, leg swings, torso twists, and a light jog, then add a few shoulder moves from the [Youth Throwers Ten program](https://ijspt.scholasticahq.com/article/29923-the-youth-throwers-ten-exercise-program-a-variation-of-an-exercise-series-for-enhanced-dynamic-shoulder-control-in-the-youth-overhead-throwing-atlet).

- Dynamic warm-up for 3 to 5 minutes
- [Band](/go/resistance-bands-set/) or bodyweight shoulder work
- First throws at half distance and half effort
- Pitch counts and rest days logged against [Pitch Smart's](https://www.usabaseball.com/news/mlb-usa-baseball-announce-updates-to-pitch-smart-program-251090688) charts
- Four months a year off overhead throwing, per [ASMI guidance](https://www.ortho.wustl.edu/mm/files/Patient%20Education/YACInjuryPreventionFlyer-2.pdf)

> Fatigue shows up before a kid says "I'm tired." Watch for a dropped elbow, a stiff upright trunk, or throws that suddenly miss. Any one of those ends throwing for the day.

The same flyer adds one or two rest days a week and a full month off from all sports each year.

## Fixing the Five Most Common Throwing Mistakes

Most young throwers share the same few flaws, and each one has a fast fix.

- **Arming the ball:** cue "push off your back leg," then rep crow-hop throws.
- **Closed front side:** cue "point your front shoulder at the target," then rep split-step throws.
- **Short stride:** cue "bigger step at your target" during quick catch.
- **Late trunk rotation:** cue "hips lead, arm follows," with 10 one-knee throws.
- **Dropped elbow:** cue "elbow up before you throw," again with the one-knee drill.

Fix one fault at a time. Kids absorb one clear cue far faster than three.

## Teaching Throwing by Age: 6 to 14

1. **Ages 6 to 8:** short, game-like catch under 20 feet, in sessions under 10 minutes.
2. **Ages 9 to 11:** add targets and the one-knee drill, building toward 30 to 45 feet.
3. **Ages 12 to 14:** add crow-hop and split-step work, building distance only while the arm stays pain-free.

Leave breaking pitches and weighted balls alone until fastball mechanics are solid and a qualified coach is involved. With the youngest group, short and fun wins every time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What's the best baseball throwing drill for beginners?**
Quick catch and one-knee throws. Both teach arm action without asking for distance.

**How often should youth players do throwing drills?**
Two or three sessions a week, with a rest day between them and a close eye on pitch counts. Follow Pitch Smart's limits for any pitching.

**How far should a 9-year-old throw during practice?**
Around 30 to 45 feet. Build up slowly, and back off at the first sign of fatigue or a dropped elbow.

**When should a young pitcher learn breaking pitches?**
Wait until fastball mechanics are solid and a qualified coach is guiding it. Growing arms come first.
