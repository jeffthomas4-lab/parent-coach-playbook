At 15 or older, "I am done" deserves a real conversation. It does not require an immediate yes, but it should not trigger a sales pitch for one more season either.

Start by asking what they want to leave. The answer might be this coach, this travel schedule, this level, or the sport itself. Those are four different decisions.

## Find the edge of the decision

Ask three questions and stop talking long enough to hear the answers:

1. If the team and schedule changed, would you still want to play?
2. Has this feeling lasted a bad week, most of the season, or longer?
3. What would you want to do with the time instead?

For a younger kid or a decision that appeared after one rough practice, use the broader guide to [sorting a hard week from a real quit](/team-parent/when-your-kid-wants-to-quit/). This page is for the older player whose answer has held up after the emotion cooled.

Do not make them defend every year the family already invested. The old fees, drives, and weekends are gone either way. The choice is about the next season.

## Let them own the choice

They own the preference. You still own the family logistics and the obligation to leave the current commitment cleanly.

Try this: "I hear that you are done. Let's take two days, make sure we understand what you are leaving, and then plan the exit together." That sentence respects the decision without turning Tuesday's frustration into Wednesday's email.

If they are leaving travel but keeping the sport, move to school, rec, pickup, or a local league. If they are leaving the sport, do not immediately assign a replacement. Let the open space exist long enough for them to choose what comes next.

## Close the chapter without a trial

Finish the season when the situation is safe and workable. When it is not, talk with the coach about an earlier exit instead of forcing your kid to absorb the rest of the calendar in silence.

Our guide to [quitting travel cleanly](/drive-home/quitting-travel-how-to-do-it-cleanly/) covers the coach and teammate conversations. If college expectations are making the choice feel bigger than it is, read [when your kid is not going to play in college](/drive-home/when-your-kid-isnt-going-to-play-in-college/) before you answer.

The last conversation can be short: "You gave this a fair run. What do you want the next month to look like?" Then put the old gear away without turning it into evidence against them later.
