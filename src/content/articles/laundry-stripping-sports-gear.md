Everyone's stripping their laundry right now. Borax, washing soda, a scoop of powder detergent, an overnight soak in the hottest water your machine can pull. It works great on white cotton sheets that have ten years of fabric softener buildup.

Wrong call for a jersey.

**Why it wrecks the gear**
Sports jerseys and practice tops are polyester blends with elastane woven in for stretch. High pH and hot water break elastane down. Strip a jersey enough times and the fit goes loose before the season's over.

**What's actually causing the smell**
It's not detergent residue. Bacteria living in the polyester weave, feeding on sweat, is the actual cause.

Fabric softener makes it worse. It coats synthetic fibers in a waxy layer that seals the bacteria in instead of letting the wash rinse it out. Skip the softener sheet and the liquid softener too.

**What gets the smell out**
Add a cup of white vinegar to the rinse cycle. Or presoak thirty minutes in a fifty-fifty mix of water and vinegar before the regular wash.

Baking soda works the same way. Half a cup in cool water for an hour, or added to the washer once it's half full so it doesn't clump against the fabric.

If the smell keeps coming back after that, switch to a detergent built for sports gear. The enzyme formulas break down the sweat proteins the bacteria feed on. Regular detergent doesn't touch that layer.

**Water temperature**
Cold or warm, never hot. Heat sets the odor into the fibers instead of releasing it, and it's the same heat that degrades the elastane.

**Drying**
Air dry when you can. High heat in the dryer does to elastane what hot water does. If it has to go in the machine, use low heat and pull it out while it's still slightly damp.

None of this takes longer than a normal wash. It just skips the borax and the overnight soak, and the jersey holds its stretch through November instead of March.
