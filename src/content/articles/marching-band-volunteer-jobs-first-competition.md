Band camp wraps in late July or the first week of August, and the first Saturday competition follows three to five weeks after. Four jobs need filling in between. None of them fill themselves.

Pit crew moves the front-ensemble equipment, the marimbas, timpani, and amp racks too heavy for a kid to carry alone. At a competition they get the truck packed before the bus call, then set the pit on the field inside the ten-minute window between bands. No experience required, just the ability to lift on a schedule.

Uniform crew fits every uniform, shoe, and pair of gauntlets during the first few days of camp. On show day they check each section for shoes tied, hair up, and hems even before anyone steps on the field. Nobody needs to know how to sew, the director teaches it in five minutes.

Chaperones ride the buses, walk the halls at overnight competitions, and pack the coolers with water and ice before every trip. Most schools require a driver's license on file with the front office before a parent can sign up, so that paperwork is worth handling early.

Feeding the band is exactly what it sounds like: getting eighty to a hundred and fifty teenagers a real meal between the last rehearsal and a competition that runs past dinner. A walking-taco line gets more actual food into a marcher than a granola bar in a backpack.

These four jobs are not optional labor. Skip one and something breaks: uniforms go on late, or the truck sits half-packed at bus call, or thirty kids stand around waiting for water. Sign up at the pre-season parent meeting, not the week of the first Saturday competition, when every slot the board posted is already gone.

Pick one job, not all four. A booster board would rather have four parents covering one role each than one parent trying to run all of them by October.
