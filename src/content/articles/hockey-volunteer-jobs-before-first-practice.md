Practices start in September, and three jobs need a parent before the puck drops on the first scrimmage. None of them are optional, and none of them can be filled the week you show up.

League scheduling meetings land in the same September window, and that is usually where the volunteer sign-up sheet gets passed around. Skip the meeting and you find out later which slot is left, if any.

A scorekeeper tracks shots, goals, and penalties on the official scoresheet for every home game. Referees and the other bench staff rely on that sheet being accurate in real time, not reconstructed after the buzzer. No hockey background is required, just attention and a pen that works.

Penalty box attendants open the door, watch the clock, and get the right player back on the ice the second a penalty clears. USA Hockey requires an adult in the box for every game, home and away. A coach running the bench cannot double as the attendant.

Locker room monitors check out the key, stay within earshot during practices and games, and help the youngest kids with skates and gear when a coach is not around. Associations require two monitors per session. Because the job puts an adult alone with other people's kids, USA Hockey treats it as a credentialed role, not a favor.

Credentialing is the part that catches families off guard. USA Hockey membership for volunteers is free, but the SafeSport course runs 90 minutes and the background check costs $30 and takes about two weeks to clear. A parent who starts that process at the first practice will not be cleared until well into the season.

Ask the team manager which slot is still open before the first practice, not after. The credentialing takes longer than the job itself.
