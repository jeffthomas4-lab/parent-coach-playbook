## 3. The post-game breakdown checklist we'd hand every rec soccer team

**Source:** newest published, 2026-08-11 — https://parentcoachdesk.com/drive-there/post-game-field-breakdown-checklist/
**Format:** checklist / list post

**Core copy:**
> The post-game breakdown checklist we'd hand every rec soccer team:
>
> - Fold the goal face down and lock it, or chain it to a fence post. Standing and unanchored isn't an option.
> - Pull the net off first. A goal with the net still on gives a kid something to climb.
> - One named parent per equipment bag, cones, water jug, first-aid kit, assigned before the season starts.
> - Two or three volunteers per game, confirmed by midweek and reminded the day before.
> - Walk the field once before the last car leaves. Check for holes, a loose base, cones left in the lot.
>
> CPSC's own guidelines count at least 21 deaths tied to unanchored soccer goals between 1979 and 1994. Most weren't during a game. The goal was standing empty when someone climbed it.

## 4. Why almost nobody gets cut from cross country

**Source:** evergreen resurface, originally published 2026-07-06 — https://parentcoachdesk.com/drive-there/why-almost-nobody-gets-cut-from-cross-country/
**Format:** carousel outline (6 slides)

**Core copy / slide outline:**
> **Slide 1 (cover):** Basketball cuts kids in November. Cross country almost never cuts anyone. Here's why that's built into the sport.
> **Slide 2:** Team scoring rewards depth. Lowest total score wins, and a slower runner who beats even one opposing runner is doing real work for the team.
> **Slide 3:** Only the varsity race caps at 7 runners. JV, reserve, and freshman races hold as many kids as show up. A big JV group becomes next year's varsity depth.
> **Slide 4:** No equipment ceiling and no field-time ceiling. A cross country team just needs more kids running. Nothing gets worse with 40 kids on the roster instead of 15.
> **Slide 5:** The tradeoff is real. No cut also means no shortcut. A varsity spot is earned by getting faster, not by a good tryout day.
> **Slide 6 (close):** If your kid isn't the fastest kid in gym class, this is one of the few sports built to keep them anyway. Full breakdown: [link]

## 5. Most parent-coaches say "I'm harder on my own kid" like a defense

**Source:** evergreen resurface, originally published 2026-05-13 — https://parentcoachdesk.com/drive-home/coaching-your-own-kid-fairly/
**Format:** short thread

**Core copy:**
> 1/ Most parent-coaches say "I'm harder on my own kid" like a defense. It's covering two opposite problems, and most of us don't know which one we're actually doing.
>
> 2/ Overcorrection: louder and longer with your own kid than with anyone else's. The team doesn't read that as fairness. They read it as you're mad at your kid.
>
> 3/ Undercorrection: you skip the correction because you don't want your kid singled out. The team notices your kid gets less feedback than everyone else, and that erodes faster.
>
> 4/ The test: ask your assistant coach, not your spouse, not your kid. Am I coaching my kid the same as the others, and if not, which direction am I off? A good assistant knows in ten seconds.
>
> 5/ Track three corrections a practice for two weeks: who, what words, how long. The pattern jumps out fast. Full piece with the fix once you know which way you're off: [link]
