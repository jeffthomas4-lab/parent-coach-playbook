## 1. Stop buying ice cream for wins only

**Source:** newest published, 2026-09-30 - https://parentcoachdesk.com/drive-home/reward-systems-youth-sports/
**Format:** tip card

**Core copy:**
> Stop buying ice cream for wins only. The reward that sticks ties to effort you can name, not the scoreboard.
>
> Try one system for six weeks before you add a second. A sticker board for hustle plays. An unsung-hero note that rotates to a different kid each week. Captain-for-a-day after a week of consistent work. Pick one. Run it. Review at week six.
>
> Age shifts the delivery. Ages 6 to 9 want fast, visible feedback. Ages 10 to 12 want roles and progress charts. Ages 13 and up want a quiet word or real responsibility, not a public sticker chart.
>
> Two scripts for the car: after a strong effort, "I saw you hustle back on defense even when you were tired." After a tough loss, "That one stung. You still played hard. Want pizza or tacos tonight?"

## 3. A ready-to-run youth hockey practice when ice time is short

**Source:** newest published, 2026-09-28 - https://parentcoachdesk.com/game/hockey-practice-plan-youth/
**Format:** checklist / list post

**Core copy:**
> A ready-to-run youth hockey practice when ice time is short:
>
> - 6U and 8U: short stations, whole group moving, fundamentals and game-like reps inside 50 to 60 minutes.
> - 10U and 12U: mix stations with small-area games so kids decide under pressure. Plan for 60 to 75 minutes.
> - Small-area games beat full-ice drills for hockey sense. More touches. Faster reads.

>

## 4. Is it favoritism, or an unclear playing-time model?

**Source:** newest published, 2026-09-27 - https://parentcoachdesk.com/game/favoritism-coaching-your-child/
**Format:** discussion-question post

**Core copy:**
> Is it favoritism, or an unclear playing-time model?
>
> If your kid sits while a teammate with similar skills keeps getting minutes, that pattern deserves a look. It does not automatically mean a hidden agenda. Most disputed lineups trace back to a missing written policy, not a secret preference.
>
> Best move today: check your team against a written policy, or write one if none exists. Document what you see before you say anything to the coach. Look for inconsistent rule application, secrecy, and defensive answers when parents ask fair questions.
>
> Safety and boundary issues are a different lane. Those get reported. Playing-time confusion gets a calm paper trail first.
>

## 6. Heat dries gear fast. It also ruins it.

**Source:** evergreen resurface (hockey / fall wet-gear season) - https://parentcoachdesk.com/team-parent/dry-the-gear-without-warping-it/
**Format:** tip card

**Core copy:**
> Heat dries gear fast. It also ruins it.
>
> Hockey skates are heat-molded once at the shop on purpose. A clothes dryer, car dashboard, or space heater delivers that heat again at home and remolds the boot to nothing useful. Bauer and CCM both warn against drying skates near direct heat.
>
> What works: low-heat boot dryers around 100 to 110 degrees, or a box fan with gear unzipped and stuffed with newspaper overnight. Airflow pulls moisture. Hard heat warps liners and softens glue.
>
> Same rule for cleats, gloves, and ski boots with custom liners. Skip the radiator. Leave the gear open to air.
>
> Full drying guide: [link]
