## 1. Wed checklist: before your child plays up an age group

**Source:** Playing Up an Age Group? A Parent Checklist Before a Full Move (published 2026-10-14) - https://parentcoachdesk.com/game/playing-up-age-group/
**Format:** gear / checklist (Wed)

**Core copy:**
> The coach asked if your kid wants to play up an age group. Run this checklist before you say yes.
>
> Get the eligibility rule in writing from the club, league, or tournament. Forfeits are the usual price when an ineligible player turns up mid-season, and the whole team pays it.
>
> Ask about the role. How many minutes per game should your child expect on the older team, and what happens if that does not hold up after a few weeks?
>
> Start with a short trial, like guest play or training up. Keep weekly training increases to 10 to 20 percent, and protect at least one rest day.
>
> Then ask your child what they want, and let that answer carry real weight before you share what you think.
>
> The full readiness checklist and the questions to ask the coach: https://parentcoachdesk.com/game/playing-up-age-group/?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=2026-10-14

## 2. Thu discussion: what do you say on the ride home after a loss?

**Source:** Car Ride Scripts: Parent Coach Playbook for a Youth Team Losing Streak (published 2026-10-07, rewritten 2026-10-12 (dek and bluf fixed)) - https://parentcoachdesk.com/team-parent/losing-streak-youth-team/
**Format:** discussion question (Thu)

**Core copy:**
> Sports parents, a question for you: what do you say on the ride home after a loss?
>
> In a USA Volleyball guide, psychologist Kevin Chapman says a few minutes of silence in the car is fine. Keep snacks and water handy, and skip blaming teammates, officials, or the coach.
>
> Once they have eaten, try one question: "What did you learn?"
>
> During a long losing streak, pick one or two behaviors your kid controls and tally those instead of the score. If low mood or avoiding practice lasts more than a couple of weeks, have a private talk, and bring in a counselor or pediatrician if it does not ease.
>
> Share your go-to line in the comments.

## 3. Fri link post: a one-page playing time policy

**Source:** Parents: Ready to Use Playing Time Policy and Scripts for Youth Sports (published 2026-10-05, rewritten 2026-10-12 (dek and bluf fixed)) - https://parentcoachdesk.com/game/playing-time-policy-youth/
**Format:** link post / checklist (Fri)

**Core copy:**
> Playing time questions go better when the policy is on paper before the first game.
>
> Match the model to the team. Younger and developmental teams do best with rotation or guaranteed minutes, while older competitive teams can use earned time with clear, written criteria.
>
> A one-page policy covers five things:
> - Team purpose, developmental or competitive
> - Minimum minutes, or the criteria for earning time
> - Attendance and effort rules
> - Who to talk to, and in what order
> - The preseason meeting date
>
> When a question comes up, the athlete talks to the coach first. Parents follow after a 24-hour cooling-off day.
>
> The policy checklist and coach-ready scripts: https://parentcoachdesk.com/game/playing-time-policy-youth/?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=2026-10-14
