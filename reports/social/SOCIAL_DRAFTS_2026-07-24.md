## 4. Two-a-days: what's in the bag

**Source:** evergreen resurface, originally published 2026-06-11 — https://parentcoachdesk.com/drive-there/two-a-days-what-parents-need-to-know/
**Format:** checklist / short list post

**Core copy:**
> Two-a-days start in the next few weeks for a lot of football programs. Here's what's actually in the bag, and two things worth knowing before day one.
>
> **Pack every day:**
> - Full water bottle plus a way to refill it
> - Their own snack for the break period (a real lunch, not a bag of chips)
> - Sunscreen
> - Any prescribed medications
>
> **Know before day one:**
> - Most states require 4-5 days in shells or helmets-only before full pads
> - Heat exhaustion: heavy sweating, nausea, cramps, lightheadedness
> - Heat stroke: hot skin, confusion, no sweating, collapsing. That's a 911 call, not a drive to the ER.
> - 8-9 hours of sleep is the floor during two-a-day week

**Platform tags:**

## 5. Hydration by the numbers

**Source:** evergreen resurface, originally published 2026-06-11 — https://parentcoachdesk.com/game/hydration-by-the-numbers/
**Format:** single tip card

**Core copy:**
> "Make sure they stay hydrated" tells you nothing. Here's one real number.
>
> A 70-pound kid needs about 35 oz of water on a normal day. Add 16 oz for every 30-60 minutes of activity. In July heat, add another 8 oz on top of that.
>
> By the time a kid feels thirsty, they're already 1-2% dehydrated, and reaction time and endurance are already dropping.
>
> 16 oz two hours before the game. 8 oz thirty minutes before. 4-8 oz every 15-20 minutes during. That's the whole formula.

**Platform tags:**
- **Facebook / X / Instagram:** same core copy works as a single card across all three. For X, trim to just the four numbers (drop the opening line), under 280 characters.
- **Instagram:** this is the natural tip-card format, caption pairs with the visual, hashtags #hydration #youthsports #sportsparents #summerheat.
- **Pinterest pin description:** "How much water your kid actually needs by weight, before, during, and after a game. Real numbers, not 'stay hydrated.' From Parent Coach Desk."

## 6. The hot-tournament bag

**Source:** evergreen resurface, originally published 2026-07-04 — https://parentcoachdesk.com/team-parent/summer-tournament-heat-safety-packing-list/
**Format:** carousel outline (8 slides)

**Core copy / slide outline:**
> **Slide 1 (cover):** What's actually in our bag by the third hot tournament of the summer.
> **Slide 2:** One gallon of water per kid per day, refilled from a cooler. Plus a second bottle just for pouring over the back of the neck.
> **Slide 3:** A cooling towel that's actually cold. We keep two, so one is always chilling in the cooler.
> **Slide 4:** Electrolyte powder packets, not sports drinks. Less sugar, same minerals, a whole box weighs less than a case of bottled drinks.
> **Slide 5:** A pop-up shade tent, if the venue allows one. Check the tournament rules first.
> **Slide 6:** A dry backup shirt for the kid, packed separate from the uniform bag.
> **Slide 7:** A small battery fan that clips to a chair. Cheap, plan to replace it every year or two.
> **Slide 8 (close):** None of this replaces your league's actual heat policy. It's what's made hot tournament days survivable for one family.

## 7. Why cross country doesn't cut anyone

**Source:** evergreen resurface, originally published 2026-07-06 — https://parentcoachdesk.com/drive-there/why-almost-nobody-gets-cut-from-cross-country/
**Format:** link post

**Core copy:**
> Basketball cuts kids in November. Football caps a roster because only so many players fit on a field. Cross country does neither.
>
> The reason is built into how the sport scores. A team's 5th, 6th, and 7th finishers can push down an opposing team's scorers without ever scoring themselves, so a program benefits from more kids training, not fewer. JV and reserve races have no cap. Only varsity limits a team to 7 entries.
>
> If your kid wants to compete but isn't fast yet, this is one of the only sports built to keep them on the team while they get there.
