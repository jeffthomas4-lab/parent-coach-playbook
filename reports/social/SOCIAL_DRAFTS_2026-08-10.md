## 2. Heat dries gear fast. It also warps it.

**Source:** newest published, 2026-08-09 — https://parentcoachdesk.com/team-parent/dry-the-gear-without-warping-it/
**Format:** tip card

**Core copy:**
> Heat dries gear fast. It also warps skates, ski boots, and cleats built with heat-molded parts.
>
> Skip the clothes dryer and the space heater. A low-heat electric dryer running near 100 to 110 degrees, or a box fan with the gear unzipped and stuffed with newspaper, does the job overnight without the damage.
>
> Free version: stuff wet cleats with newspaper and swap it out every few hours. Turn gloves inside out so the liner meets the air. Pull the blade guards off skates and stand them tongue-down on a towel.
>
> A radiator dries a cleat faster. It also melts the glue holding the sole on before the cleat plays another game.

**Platform tags:**
- **Facebook:** full copy. Timely as fall gear comes out of summer storage damp or as two-a-days start soaking gear daily.
- **X:** trim: "Heat dries gear fast, but it warps skates, ski boots, and cleats with heat-molded parts. Use a low-heat dryer near 100-110 degrees, or a box fan with the bag unzipped and newspaper stuffed inside. A radiator dries a cleat faster and melts the sole glue doing it." + link.

## 3. One color per kid, not one bin per sport.

**Source:** newest published, 2026-08-09 — https://parentcoachdesk.com/team-parent/color-coded-bin-system-for-multi-sport-families/
**Format:** tip card

**Core copy:**
> Three kids, three sports, one hallway by the back door. A swim cap turns up in the baseball bag two weeks later, dried stiff and forgotten.
>
> Sport-based color systems don't survive a season change. Pick a color per kid instead. One color, every sport, all year.
>
> Buy each kid a bin in their color and a roll of matching duct tape. Wrap a strip around water bottles, mouthguard cases, and the zipper pull on a bag that looks like six other black bags on the same rack.
>
> A kid who can match blue to blue can run this alone by age seven or eight.

**Platform tags:**

## 4. Cross country training runs 20 to 35 miles a week. Most of it should feel easy.

**Source:** evergreen resurface, originally published 2026-06-11 — https://parentcoachdesk.com/drive-there/cross-country-training-guide-parents/
**Format:** link post

**Core copy:**
> Cross country training for 13-14 year olds runs 20 to 35 miles a week: one longer run on the weekend, one or two workout days, the rest easy conversational pace.
>
> The 80-20 model puts 80 percent of training at low intensity. Easy days are supposed to be easy. That's not laziness. That's how distance running works.
>
> The real risk is mileage jumping too fast off summer break. The standard guideline caps weekly mileage increases at 10 percent. A kid going from summer break straight to 40 miles in week one of practice is a stress-injury risk, not a fast start.
>
> What you can actually do: protect sleep, leave the pace conversation to the coach, and check the shoes aren't worn out before the mileage builds.

## 5. First season of youth soccer: what nobody tells you before the first game.

**Source:** evergreen resurface, originally published 2026-06-11 — https://parentcoachdesk.com/drive-there/first-season-of-youth-soccer/
**Format:** carousel outline (6 slides)

**Core copy / slide outline:**
> **Slide 1 (cover):** First season of youth soccer? Here's what nobody tells you before the first game.
> **Slide 2:** U6 plays 3v3, U8 plays 4v4 or 5v5. No goalkeeper until U10. The swarm around the ball is the design, not a mistake.
> **Slide 3:** Shin guards aren't optional at any age. Hard-shell with a sleeve underneath, $15 to $25, on before every practice.
> **Slide 4:** Ball sizes by age: size 3 for U8 and under, size 4 through U12, size 5 from U12 up. Write your kid's name on it before day one.
> **Slide 5:** Cleats or turf shoes both work at rec level, $25 to $50, and last about two seasons.
> **Slide 6 (close):** Total first-year cost at rec level: $100 to $180, on top of $80 to $150 registration. The rest sorts itself out.

## 7. What does youth tennis actually cost?

**Source:** seasonal (SEASONAL_PLAN_2026-08.md social queue, tennis — nationals-window travel costs timely now), originally published 2026-06-11 — https://parentcoachdesk.com/drive-there/youth-tennis-cost-breakdown/
**Format:** checklist / list post

**Core copy:**
> What does youth tennis actually cost? It depends how far the competitive track goes. The spread is one of the widest in youth sport.
>
> - Group lessons: $12-25 a session, $50-150 a month. The right starting point at any age.
> - Private lessons: $50-120 an hour. Twice a week adds $400-1,000 a month.
> - Tournament play: $25-75 per USTA entry. Two tournaments a month plus travel adds $100-400 a month.
> - Academy training: $1,000-3,000 a month for full-day programs. Residential academies run $30,000-60,000 a year.
> - Strings and gear: restringing runs $25-60, twice a month for serious players. Racket upgrades and shoes add $300-600 a year.
>
> Recreational to moderately competitive tennis runs $3,000 to $8,000 a year, all in.
