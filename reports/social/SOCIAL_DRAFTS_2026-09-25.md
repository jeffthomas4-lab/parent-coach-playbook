## 1. Start new volleyball servers 10 feet from the net, not the end line

**Source:** newest published, 2026-09-25 - https://parentcoachdesk.com/game/youth-volleyball-serving-drills/
**Format:** carousel outline (6 slides)

**Core copy / slide outline:**
> **Slide 1 (cover):** Start every new server about 10 feet from the net, not on the end line. Drill the toss before you ever add a swing.
> **Slide 2:** Three drills that earn a spot every practice: toss-and-drop, target serving, and serve-and-chase.
> **Slide 3:** Younger kids stay underhand until coordination catches up. Overhand comes later, once contact and footwork are reliable.
> **Slide 4:** Progress by success rate, not the calendar. USA Volleyball junior plans start near the 3-meter line and move back only after consistent makes (about 8 of 10 legal).
> **Slide 5:** Diagnose bad serves in this order: toss, footwork, contact point, follow-through. A bad toss ruins everything else.
> **Slide 6 (close):** Pick two or three drills for tomorrow. Age notes and the full six-drill bank: [link]

## 2. Three postgame moves that raise motivation tonight

**Source:** newest published, 2026-09-18 - https://parentcoachdesk.com/drive-home/motivating-kids-in-sports/
**Format:** tip card

**Core copy:**
> Three postgame moves that raise motivation tonight. No pep talk required.
>
> 1. Ask one reflective question: "What was the toughest part of that game?" Then wait.
> 2. Name a specific effort you saw: "You kept your feet moving the whole third set." Process praise beats scoreboard praise.
> 3. Hand them one small choice about tomorrow: warm-up first or stretch first, which drill, which skill.
>
> Autonomy-supportive parenting is not backing off. It is giving input while handing real decisions back. If a phrase lands flat, adjust next time instead of repeating what did not work.
>
> Age notes and ready scripts: [link]

## 3. Does your kid dread the car ride home, or look forward to it?

**Source:** newest published, 2026-09-11 - https://parentcoachdesk.com/drive-home/post-game-talk/
**Format:** discussion-question post

**Core copy:**
> Does your kid dread the car ride home, or look forward to it?
>
> The right postgame talk starts before you say a single word: a hug, "I love watching you play," or "Great effort, want to talk about it or just chill for a bit?" Support first. Coach later.
>
> If they are silent, teary, or turned toward the window, that silence is the answer. Give the space.
>
> After a win, name one concrete behavior ("You kept encouraging teammates when it got close"), not a highlight reel. After a loss, "That one stung, huh?" and then quiet is often enough.
>
> Same greeting, same tone, same questions after wins and losses. That habit protects worth from the scoreboard.
>
> What is the first thing you usually say when the doors close?

## 4. Fall crew is a head race, not a six-lane sprint

**Source:** newest published, 2026-09-16 - https://parentcoachdesk.com/drive-there/what-a-head-race-actually-is/
**Format:** link post

**Core copy:**
> Fall crew is a head race, not a six-lane sprint. If you picture a shared start gun and boats blasting off together, you are watching the wrong season.
>
> A head race is a time trial over roughly 4,000 to 5,000 meters. Boats leave one after another, often ten to twenty seconds apart, and race the clock. The result sheet ranks elapsed time, not who crossed a shared finish line first.
>
> You will not know if your kid's boat is "winning" until the times post. Bring coffee and patience.
>
> USRowing treats fall and spring as different shapes on purpose. Fall builds aerobic base. Spring is the championship sprint season at 2,000 meters.
>
> Next Saturday, when the first boat pushes off alone, you will know what you are watching. Full parent guide: [link]

## 5. A 30 to 45 minute youth goalie block you can run tomorrow

**Source:** newest published, 2026-09-14 - https://parentcoachdesk.com/team-parent/youth-goalie-drills/
**Format:** short thread

**Core copy:**
> 1/ A 30 to 45 minute youth goalie block covers five buckets: footwork, handling, reactive diving, distribution, and cross-claims. You can hit all five in one session.
>
> 2/ Sample clock: 0-5 activation with a ball, 5-15 handling and set position, 15-25 footwork-to-save, then distribution and a short game block. Cue: "hands soft, body behind the ball."
>
> 3/ US Youth Soccer: rotate kids through goalkeeper through age 12. Teach keeper fundamentals around U9 to U10, but do not specialize before U14.
>
> 4/ Age focus shifts. U6-U8: confidence and basic handling only. U9-U10: footwork and scoop catching. U11-U12: distribution and angle play over raw reaction speed.
>
> 5/ Safety first: anchored goals, safe landing technique, and enough rest between diving reps. Full drill bank and printable plan: [link]

## 6. After a volleyball match, say this first

**Source:** evergreen resurface (fall volleyball season) - https://parentcoachdesk.com/drive-home/after-a-volleyball-match-what-to-say/
**Format:** tip card

**Core copy:**
> After a volleyball match, say this first: "I loved watching you out there today." Not a recap. Not "you had a rough one." Just that. Then wait.
>
> Questions that open things up: "What was your favorite play?" or "How did that last set feel?" Invite without demanding.
>
> Skip these in the car: "Your serve was off in the second set." "Why didn't you call that ball?" "The setter wasn't giving you enough looks." Those are coaching notes, not parent comments.
>
> After a tough match: "That one stung, huh?" and silence is enough. After a great one: keep the energy proportional. "You were locked in on defense tonight" beats a five-minute highlight reel.
>
> The rule that holds: your kid should look forward to the car ride home. Full guide: [link]

## 7. The snack rotation that does not fall apart by week four

**Source:** evergreen resurface (team-parent checklist, fall rec season) - https://parentcoachdesk.com/team-parent/the-snack-rotation-that-doesnt-fall-apart/
**Format:** checklist / list post

**Core copy:**
> Most rec snack rotations fall apart by week four because nobody knows whose turn it is. The format that holds:
>
> - One shared spreadsheet. Twelve rows for twelve weeks. Two columns: date and family.
> - Send it week one: "Please pick a week. First-come on the calendar."
> - Snack rule: two food items, one drink. No candy mandate. No impressiveness contest.
> - Wednesday morning reminder to the Saturday family only, not the whole team chat: "You're up for snack this Saturday at the 9am game."
> - Backup: team manager keeps a $15 store gift card. Forgot family takes the next open week. No drama.
>
> Simple beats clever. Full checklist: [link]
