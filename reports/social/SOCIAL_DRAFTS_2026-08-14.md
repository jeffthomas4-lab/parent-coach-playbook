## 2. Two numbers that replace guesswork on a hot or stormy sideline

**Source:** newest published, 2026-08-07 — https://parentcoachdesk.com/team-parent/heat-lightning-decision-trees/
**Format:** tip card

**Core copy:**
> Two numbers replace guesswork on a hot or stormy sideline.
>
> Heat: most state associations run the call off Wet Bulb Globe Temperature, not the thermometer. At 87 WBGT, football drops to helmet, shoulder pads, and shorts, capped at two hours. Cross 92 and nobody practices outside, full stop.
>
> Lightning: count from flash to thunder. Thirty seconds or fewer means the strike hit within six miles, and that's when everyone goes inside for at least 30 minutes, with a new strike resetting the clock to zero.
>
> Check your own league's written heat policy for its exact cutoffs. The number does the deciding so nobody has to guess.

**Platform tags:**
- **Facebook:** full copy. Timely as August practices run hot and afternoon storms are common.

## 4. The first-practice checklist we'd hand a brand-new coach

**Source:** evergreen resurface, originally published 2026-06-13 — https://parentcoachdesk.com/team-parent/running-your-first-practice/
**Format:** checklist / list post

**Core copy:**
> The first-practice checklist we'd hand a brand-new coach:
>
> - Show up 20 minutes early. Know where the equipment, bathrooms, and gathering spot are before a single kid arrives.
> - Skip the speech. Get them moving in the first 15 minutes instead, something with no wrong answers.
> - Build the middle 20 to 25 minutes around one skill, broken into small steps so every kid gets a win.
> - Scrimmage at the end and let them play. Resist coaching from the sideline every 30 seconds.
> - Close with two specific things you saw: one kid's name and what they did, one team-wide note. Then tell them what's next.
>
> Your first practice doesn't need to be good. It needs to end with the kids wanting to come back.

**Platform tags:**

## 5. Your 11-year-old barely touched the water bottle

**Source:** evergreen resurface, originally published 2026-04-23 — https://parentcoachdesk.com/drive-there/the-hydration-conversation-at-11-12/
**Format:** short thread

**Core copy:**
> 1/ Your 11-year-old finishes a game having barely touched the water bottle, then looks more wiped out than 60 minutes should explain. That's usually dehydration, not fitness.
>
> 2/ Kids this age don't feel thirsty until they're already behind on fluids. By the time they ask for water, the deficit is already real.
>
> 3/ The actual numbers: 10 to 15 ounces before the game, 5 to 10 ounces every 15 minutes during if you can manage it, 20 ounces in the first 30 minutes after.
>
> 4/ Water covers about 90 percent of it. Save the sports drink for practices over 60 minutes or the hottest days.
>
> 5/ Watch for dark urine, a headache after practice, or being unusually tired for the effort. Those are the signs the habit isn't there yet, not a toughness problem.
