## 1. Did your club hand you a written season-cost sheet before you signed?

**Source:** newest published, 2026-09-23 - https://parentcoachdesk.com/game/youth-sports-budget/

## 2. The pregame talk that works is 15 to 45 seconds

**Source:** newest published, 2026-09-16 - https://parentcoachdesk.com/drive-there/pregame-talk-youth-sports/
**Format:** tip card

**Core copy:**
> The pregame talk that works is 15 to 45 seconds. Longer usually adds nerves, not focus.

> Car ride (about 30 seconds): "I love watching you play. Whatever happens today, I'm proud of you. One thing to focus on: hustle back on defense. Have fun out there."

> Sideline whisper (about 15 seconds): "You've got this. Loose shoulders, quick feet. Go have fun."

> Anxious kid (about 25 seconds): "It's okay to feel nervous. That means you care. Take one deep breath. Just focus on the next play, not the whole game."

> Say less than you planned. If you rehearsed three points, cut it to one. Full age-by-age scripts: [link]

## 4. Three hockey jobs need a parent before puck drop

**Source:** newest published, 2026-09-14 - https://parentcoachdesk.com/team-parent/hockey-volunteer-jobs-before-first-practice/
**Format:** checklist / list post

**Core copy:**
> Three hockey jobs need a parent before puck drop. None are optional. None can be filled the week of the first game.

> - Scorekeeper: tracks shots, goals, and penalties on the official sheet in real time.
> - Penalty box attendant: USA Hockey requires an adult in the box every game, home and away.
> - Locker room monitor: associations usually want two per session, within earshot, helping the youngest kids with gear.

> Credentialing is what catches families: free USA Hockey volunteer membership, a 90-minute SafeSport course, and a $30 background check that takes about two weeks to clear.

> Ask the team manager which slot is open before the first practice, not after. Full role guide: [link]

## 5. Ask this one question before you say anything else after the game

**Source:** newest published, 2026-09-21 - https://parentcoachdesk.com/drive-home/confidence-for-youth-athletes/
**Format:** short thread

**Core copy:**
> 1/ Ask this one question before you say anything else after the game: "What was one thing you got better at today?" Then wait. Let your kid answer first.

> 2/ Confidence grows from effort-focused feedback, small choices they own, and belonging on the team. Not from a harder pep talk in the car.

> 3/ Praise the process out loud, not just the scoreboard. "You kept your feet moving on defense" beats "great win" or "tough loss."

> 4/ Once a week, hand them one training choice: which drill, which skill, which position to practice. Autonomy compounds over a season.

> 5/ If the program publicly criticizes kids or ranks everything by outcome, that climate is working against confidence. Notice it early. Scripts and age notes: [link]

**Core copy / slide outline:**

> **Slide 2:** Warm-up (5 min): pass and move in pairs. Early touches, no standing around.
> **Slide 3:** Technical block (10 min): triangle passing or passing gates. Watch pass quality, not speed.
> **Slide 4:** Conditioned drill (10 min): two-touch squares or one-touch rondo. Cue body angle and first touch before the ball arrives.

## 7. If you suspect a concussion, the next move is removal, not a same-day return

**Source:** newest published, 2026-09-13 - https://parentcoachdesk.com/game/concussion-protocol-youth-sports/
**Format:** tip card

**Core copy:**
> If you suspect a concussion, the next move is removal from play. Not a same-day return. Not "one more series."

> Watch for danger signs that need emergency care: one pupil larger than the other, repeated vomiting, a headache that keeps getting worse, inability to wake up, weakness on one side, slurred speech or seizures, any loss of consciousness.

> Absent those red flags: remove the athlete, monitor for at least 30 minutes, notify the parent, write down what happened while it is fresh. Recognition and removal are the sideline job. Diagnosis and clearance belong to a healthcare provider.

> CDC HEADS UP is the foundation most youth programs borrow from. Full sideline scripts and return-to-play steps: [link]
