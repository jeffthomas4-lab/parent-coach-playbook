## Lead

October is doing two things at once. Fall sports are midseason: Friday night football, XC invitationals, soccer district games, marching band competitions that swallow every Saturday. And the winter calendar is already loud.

Club volleyball clinics are open before November tryouts. Hockey is weeks into the travel season. The first ECNL fall showcase cluster for a lot of age groups lands October 10 to 12.

That second half is the one that sneaks up on people. Someone in the parent chat calls the showcase "the tryout." Someone else starts pricing clinic packages like the roster depends on showing up three times this month.

Neither claim is how these weeks actually work.

We wrote into that gap this week. What an ECNL fall event really is (games under recruiting eyes, not a personal audition). Serving drills worth running at a volleyball clinic before club tryouts, plus a midseason read on reward systems that favor effort over the scoreboard.

Three reads below. Plus one from the archive for the bleachers, because November tryouts are close enough to start watching how the drills actually get scored.

**1. What an ECNL fall event actually is, and what the coach with the clipboard is doing**
An ECNL fall event is a scheduled league weekend where college coaches watch club matches. They are not running drills, and they are not there to meet parents. Treat it as competitive games under recruiting eyes, not a personal tryout.

**2. 6 Youth Volleyball Serving Drills to Build Parent Coach Confidence**
Start new servers about 10 feet from the net, drill the toss before the swing, and keep the progression short. Toss-and-drop, target serving, and serve-and-chase earn their spot before club clinics turn into November tryouts.
https://parentcoachdesk.com/game/youth-volleyball-serving-drills/
