## Lead

Mid-October is when families start asking the bigger questions. The season is far enough along that you know how it's going, and spring registration emails are already landing.

So the questions change. Should my kid play up next season, and is this the year we leave the club? And for cross country families, the Saturday meet that used to be an open invitational is about to become a cut list.

None of these get decided in the car on the way home from a loss. They get decided with the rules in writing, a real look at minutes, and a straight answer about who advances. (That last one matters more than any split time.)

Three reads below. Start with playing up if a coach has floated it, because "sure, we'll take her" is not the same as "she'll play." Plus one from the archive for the conversation that usually comes right before a family decides to move.

## Content links

**1. When playing up an age group makes sense**
Ahead technically, fine socially, and real minutes on the new team, not a bench seat. A short trial through guest play is the safer first move, and the rules go in writing first.
https://parentcoachdesk.com/game/playing-up-age-group/

**2. When to switch teams, and what to check before you call**
Leave for a pattern, not one bad season. Confirm whether a signed commitment blocks the move, and price out fees, travel, and gear before anyone says yes.
https://parentcoachdesk.com/game/switching-teams-youth-sports/

**3. District, regional, state: what each cross country round means for your Saturday**
Your state association runs the postseason, and the round names change across borders. Ask who advances as a team and who advances alone, then plan travel around that cut.
https://parentcoachdesk.com/drive-there/district-regional-state-xc-saturday/
