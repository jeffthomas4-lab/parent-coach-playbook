## 1. src/content/articles/youth-hockey-recruiting-guide.md

NCAA put a new Age-Based Eligibility Model in place as of August 2026: a Division I player's five-year eligibility clock now starts at first full-time college enrollment OR the fall semester after their 19th birthday, whichever comes first. Separately, DI men's hockey contact-period timing shifted (coaches can start recruiting conversations at 12pm ET on Sept 1 of junior year, not midnight as before; for the Class of 2028 the actual first contact day is Sept 9, 2026, since it's pegged to Labor Day).

The piece's framing — "most players who end up at Division I programs play one to three years of junior hockey first" and treats that runway as basically free — doesn't account for the new eligibility clock. A player who plays multiple junior seasons after turning 19 could now be burning DI eligibility years before ever suiting up for a college team. That's a real change to the math a 15-17-year-old's family needs to do, not a footnote.

Proposed direction: add a section (or at minimum a clear paragraph) explaining the age-based eligibility clock and telling families to check a recruit's exact eligibility math with the NCAA Eligibility Center or an advisor before committing to multiple junior seasons — not just note "academic eligibility runs through the NCAA Eligibility Center" as the piece does now. This changes the substance of the advice, so it's out of scope for a mechanical pass.
