Substantive issues found while refreshing 10 posts. None of these were edited in-file; they need a judgment call from Jeff or Ed.

## 1. src/content/body/youth-cheer-stunt-safety-briefing.md

AACCA (American Association of Cheerleading Coaches and Administrators) merged into USA Cheer in March 2018. The dead `aacca.org` link is fixed (mechanical). But the body text still treats AACCA as a live, distinct certifying body in several places:

- "USA Cheer's stunt progression rules, AACCA safety standards..."
- "Programs led by USA Cheer or AACCA-certified coaches..."
- Parent-question list: "Are your coaches USA Cheer or AACCA certified, and how recently?"
- Red flags: "Programs that lack USA Cheer or AACCA certification for at least one coach."
- "For coaches" list: "USA Cheer or AACCA certification at start of every season."

Proposed direction: either drop "AACCA" as a separate credential and just say "USA Cheer certified" throughout, or add a one-line note that AACCA is now administered under USA Cheer, so a parent doesn't go looking for a separate "AACCA certification" that no longer exists as its own thing. This is a structural/voice-touching edit across multiple passages, out of scope for a mechanical pass.

## 2. src/content/body/volunteer-vs-paid-coach-vetting.md

The piece states plainly that "AAU's Youth Protection Policy applies to all member coaches and contractors" (link fixed to AAU's renamed "Athlete Protection and Abuse Prevention Policy"). ProPublica has reported that AAU promised reforms to its child-protection practices and largely didn't implement them ("A Youth Sports Giant Promised Reforms to Protect Kids From Sexual Abuse. Most Never Happened."). The piece's framing — that a written AAU policy is meaningful reassurance — doesn't account for that enforcement gap.

Proposed direction: consider adding a line noting that a written policy isn't the same as enforcement, and that parents should ask leagues how the policy is actually checked (not just whether it exists). This cuts toward the piece's existing thesis (vetting gaps are real) rather than against it, but it's a substantive addition, not a mechanical one.
